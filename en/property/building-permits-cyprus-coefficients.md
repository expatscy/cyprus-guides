> **Short answer:** Cyprus building permits depend on the plot’s planning zone, density, coverage and height limits. Processing times depend on the procedure and completeness of the application.

## What are the two key coefficients?

### Building Coefficient (Συντελεστής Δόμησης)

Limits the **floor area counted for density purposes** under the applicable planning rules. Conditional exclusions mean this is not simply the gross area of every floor. The coefficient can be expressed as a decimal or percentage.

**Formula:**
```
Plot Area × Coefficient = Maximum Counted Floor Area
```

**Hypothetical example:** A 1,000 m² plot × coefficient 0.8 = **800 m² of counted floor area**. Arithmetically, this could be divided into two floors of 400 m² or four floors of 200 m², but only if the permitted height, storeys, coverage, setbacks and other permission conditions also allow that design.

### Coverage Ratio (Ποσοστό Κάλυψης)

Limits the **percentage of the plot covered by the building's horizontal projection**, subject to the applicable counting rules and conditional exclusions.

**Hypothetical example:** A 1,000 m² plot × 50% coverage = **500 m² maximum counted coverage**, subject to the other planning conditions.

### The Difference Between Them

| Parameter | Building Coefficient | Coverage Ratio |
|-----------|---------------------|----------------|
| What it limits | Floor area counted under density rules | Horizontal projection counted under coverage rules |
| Hypothetical example (1,000 m², 0.8 / 50%) | Up to 800 m² counted floor area | Up to 500 m² counted coverage |
| Height factor | Distribution between floors must also comply with height and storey limits | Coverage does not replace height and storey limits |

### Where to Check Your Coefficients

Use the plot identifiers to ask the competent Planning Authority for the current Development Plan, planning zone and applicable conditions. Use **IPPODAMOS** for planning applications. Do not treat a title document alone as confirmation of the current development limits.

### Typical Values by Zone

Check the applicable Local Plan and the plot’s current planning zone for density, coverage, height and storey limits. Generic residential/commercial ranges are not a reliable substitute for those rules.

## Can You Change the Coefficients?

Check the applicable Development Plan before proposing a change. An **EA10 application for planning permission by exception** is available for a proposal that does not comply with the plan; approval is not automatic.

Conditional density incentives also exist. For example, **Ministerial Order 4/2025** provides energy-performance incentives subject to its development-type, energy and other requirements. Ask the designer to assess the actual applicable conditions.

### Transfer of Development Rights (TDR)

Cyprus has a mechanism for transferring development rights from **listed/preserved donor buildings** to authorised receiving plots, subject to ministerial permission and the receiving-area rules:

- Legal basis: Town Planning Law (90/1972), Preserved Buildings Law (240(I)/2002)
- Republic contribution under **section 15**: 10% of the value of transferred remaining density, paid by the receiving owner; for granted density, 10% is withheld from the section 14 deposit
- After the statutory payments, the Director informs the Department of Lands and Surveys so the removal/addition of density is recorded in the land registers and ownership certificates of the donor and receiver properties (section 9)

## What setbacks from plot boundaries apply?

Setbacks and boundary-building permissions depend on the applicable Development Plan, whether the building is detached or continuous, and the type of ancillary structure. Obtain plot-specific confirmation before designing; a single national table of 3 m, 10 m or 1.5 m rules cannot determine your permission.

**Important:** Specific setbacks depend on the zone, Local Plan, and local regulations. Always verify with the Town Planning Authority.

## What if my neighbour is building illegally?

### Where to Complain

Report the suspected breach to the competent planning/building authority with the plot details and a description of the work. The government provides an **IPPODAMOS requests, complaints and suggestions** service. Building-permit applications are now handled by the competent **District Local Government Organisation (EOA)**; do not assume the former urban/rural office split identifies the responsible authority.

### Possible Sanctions

Under **Cap. 96, section 20**, offences can lead to criminal penalties. On conviction for an offence under section 20(1), a court may order demolition/removal or termination of unauthorised use, subject to the statutory conditions. Section 20(3A) also allows a court to suspend further work while the charge is being heard. These outcomes depend on the case and procedure.

### Neighbour and shared-property rights

Before alterations affecting shared structures, drainage or access, have the designer and legal adviser check the relevant property rights and approvals. For jointly owned buildings, **Cap. 224, section 38Δ** restricts alterations affecting other owners' rights, common property or the building's structure. This does not establish a universal neighbour-consent rule for every project.

## What is the permit process?

### Step 1: Planning Permit

- Submitted through the **Town Planning Authority** (online via IPPODAMOS)
- If no decision is issued within **three months**, the authority may ask the applicant to agree to an extension; this is not a completion guarantee
- Valid for **three years unless the permission states another period**
- May require: environmental impact assessment, transport assessment

### Step 2: Building Permit

- Submitted electronically via **IPPODAMOS** to the competent **District Local Government Organisation (EOA)**
- Supporting documents depend on the project type and size; the mandatory documents are displayed in IPPODAMOS
- The official indicative list includes architectural and property plans, structural/electrical/mechanical studies and energy calculations, where applicable
- The listed ETEK certificate concerns the designer’s **professional liability insurance**; confirm the documents required for the actual application

### For Companies — Additional Requirements

For a company’s planning application, the official list includes the Certificate of Incorporation and certificate of directors and secretary. Where there is more than one director, it also calls for a letter identifying the authorised signatory or signatories. Check the portal’s requirements for the specific application.

## What are the frequently asked questions?

**Can verandas/balconies exceed the coverage ratio?**
Balconies and verandas have separate density and coverage counting rules, with conditional exclusions under Ministerial Order 1/2017. Their treatment depends on their dimensions, design and use. Have the project designer calculate both limits under the applicable Development Plan and order; an exclusion from one calculation does not grant permission to exceed another limit.

**Can you build right up to the fence?**
Check the applicable plot-specific setbacks and boundary-building conditions. Neither a garage nor a window-free wall automatically permits construction on the boundary.

**Where are the coefficients stated — in the title or elsewhere?**
Ask the competent Planning Authority to confirm the current Development Plan, planning zone and plot-specific limits. A title document alone does not establish the current permissible design.
