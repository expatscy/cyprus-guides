> **Short answer:** Direct SWIFT from Cyprus to Russia is blocked. Working routes in 2026: Profee (if you have a non-sanctioned Russian card), third-country corridors through Kazakhstan (e-Residency or in-person) or Armenia (works both directions), or licensed services like KwikPay. Budget 1.5–5% in fees depending on the method.


You live and work in Cyprus — but you still have a mortgage on a property in Russia. Direct bank transfers no longer go through. What are your options? We break down the legal routes, the risks of grey-area workarounds, and the tax implications.

> **The situation:** You have one income — your Cyprus salary. The problem is getting money to Russia to pay the mortgage. This is **not** a dual-income issue and **not** about tax residency — it's a money transfer logistics problem.

## Why do direct transfers not work?

Since February 2022, the banking infrastructure between Cyprus and Russia has been dismantled:

- **Bank of Cyprus** closed **20,000+ accounts** belonging to ~7,000 Russian nationals
- Across all Cypriot banks: **13,000+ clients, 35,000+ accounts** closed, €2bn in exposure reduced
- SWIFT transfers to major Russian banks (Sberbank, VTB, Alfa-Bank, Otkritie) are **blocked** under EU sanctions
- In July 2025, the EU extended restrictions to a **full transaction ban** on 45+ Russian banks — not just SWIFT, but any operation

### Direct SWIFT from Cyprus to Russia — doesn't work

Even banks that formally remained outside the SWIFT ban (Gazprombank, UniCredit Russia, OTP Russia) **do not accept incoming transfers from the EU** in practice:

- **Gazprombank** — hit by full transaction ban (July 2025). Inbound SWIFT from EU blocked
- **Revolut, Wise, N26** — block outgoing transfers to Russian banks at their own compliance level
- **Bank of Cyprus, Eurobank** — will not initiate SWIFT to Russia
- Opening a Russian bank account **remotely from abroad** — impossible at most banks

**Bottom line:** a direct bank transfer Cyprus → Russia in 2026 **does not exist** as a working channel. You need alternative routes.

## How do I make the payment if I have funds in Russia?

If your Russian bank account still has a balance (savings, asset sales), payment is technically simple: Sberbank Online, T-Bank, Alfa-Click all work from abroad. You need a Russian phone number and sometimes a VPN (Sberbank periodically blocks EU logins).

You can also issue a **power of attorney** to a relative or friend in Russia — through the Russian Consulate in Nicosia (Ag. Prokopiou St, Engomi), ~€30–50, valid up to 3 years. They pay from **your** account — no questions about the source of funds.

### 1. Third-country corridors

Transfer funds via an intermediate bank account in a country that accepts transfers from both the EU and Russia. Legal when done correctly. There is no single "best" route — it depends on whether you can travel, your budget, and which direction you need money to move. Three working paths:

**Path A — Kazakhstan via e-Residency (remote, new 2026).** The official [eresidency.gov.kz](https://eresidency.gov.kz/en) programme offers a digital residency for foreigners 18+ (no nationality restrictions). Fee: $499/year promo ($999/year regular), paid by card or USDT/USDC. You get an IIN (individual ID number), a digital ID card, and a Kazakhstan eSIM. Remote bank account opening is currently available at **Freedom Bank** and **RBK Bank**; **BCC** and **Bereke Bank** were planned for January 2026 — verify status on the official site before relying on them. Banks run their own KYC/AML, so eligibility is not guaranteed. Route: Cyprus salary → SWIFT to your KZ account → convert → send to your Russian card.

**Path B — Kazakhstan in person.** Open an account during a visit to Almaty or Astana (remote opening for non-residents outside e-Residency has been closed since January 2025). Banks: Halyk, Kaspi, Freedom Finance. One-off cost is flight + 1–2 days; no annual subscription. Suits anyone who travels to Kazakhstan anyway.

**Path C — Armenia, works both directions.** Armenian banks open accounts for Russian nationals (usually in-person in Yerevan, 1–2 hours) and process SWIFT both to Russia and to the EU — a rare single channel that closes both directions at once. Banks with RUB accounts and direct correspondent links to Russia: **Ameriabank, Acba, Ardshinbank, Inecobank**. Fee 1–3% per leg (up to 5% with double conversion). Evocabank previously supported instant Tinkoff transfers but that channel was suspended in 2025 — verify current status before using.

Fee across all three paths: 2–5% per leg.

### 2. Cryptocurrency (P2P) — grey area

Not formally illegal, but carries AML risks on both sides. **Not recommended as a primary channel.**

### 3. Payment services

Licensed services that transfer EUR → RUB directly to Russian cards. Fees: 2.5–10%.

### 4. Early repayment

Under Russian law, **early mortgage repayment carries no penalties**. Cleanest exit if funds allow.

## Why is the friends scheme risky?

A common workaround: you give cash to a friend in Cyprus, and they transfer money from their Russian bank account to pay your mortgage.

### How Banks See It

**Cyprus bank:** regular cash withdrawals or transfers to acquaintances with no economic purpose → suspicious activity.

**Russian bank:** regular payments to your mortgage from unrelated third parties → who are these people and why are they paying your mortgage?

### Cyprus AML Risks (Law 188(I)/2007)

- **Cash over €10,000** — mandatory report to the Central Bank
- **Cash over €10,000 for goods/services** — **banned** since December 2024
- **Any suspicious activity** — SAR to MOKAS (no minimum threshold)
- Structuring (breaking into amounts below €10,000) — **itself** a money laundering indicator

**Penalties:** up to **14 years imprisonment** and/or **€500,000** fine.

### Law 149(I)/2025 — Sanctions Evasion

Since 25 July 2025, Cyprus criminalises:
- Providing funds to sanctioned persons
- **Any scheme to circumvent sanctions**
- **Assisting** prohibited financial operations

**Penalty:** up to **5 years imprisonment** + fine up to **€100,000**. This applies to the friend who makes the transfer too.

### Russian Risks (115-FZ)

- Transfers over RUB 100,000 require full identification
- Rosfinmonitoring can **freeze suspicious funds for 10 business days** without a court order (since June 2025)
- Suspicious patterns: 10+ counterparties/day, 50+/month; less than 1 minute between receiving and sending; withdrawing 30–40%+ of incoming amounts as cash

## What are my tax rights?

Cash "gifts" between individuals are technically NDFL-free (Art. 217 Russian Tax Code). But regular same-amount transfers on mortgage payment dates are a red flag for Rosfinmonitoring. Tax authorities can reclassify as taxable income.

The EU sanctions exemption for "basic needs" (mortgage, rent) formally exists — but in practice banks refuse anyway due to their own compliance policies.
