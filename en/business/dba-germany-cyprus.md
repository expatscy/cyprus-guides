> **In short:** The Germany-Cyprus double tax treaty prevents double taxation. Different types of pension follow different rules; Germany may tax statutory social insurance pensions. Dividends: withholding tax of 15% at most.

The double tax treaty between Germany and Cyprus (in German: DBA) determines which country may tax which income — and prevents you from paying tax on the same income in both countries. For German emigrants in Cyprus, the treaty is one of the most important documents. Here we explain it article by article.

## What is a double tax treaty and why do you need it?

A double tax treaty is an international agreement between two states. It regulates:

- **Which country** may tax which income (allocation of taxing rights)
- **How double taxation is avoided** (exemption or credit)
- **Which withholding tax rates** apply (often lower than under national law)

Without a treaty, Germany would tax your worldwide income (as long as you are resident there), and Cyprus would do the same — a double tax burden. The treaty prevents that.

## What is the current status of the treaty?

| Date | Event |
|-------|----------|
| 9 May 1974 | Original treaty signed |
| 18 February 2011 | **New version** signed in Nicosia (replaces the 1974 treaty) |
| 16 December 2011 | New version entered into force |
| 19 February 2021 | **Amending protocol** signed (anti-abuse clause and changes to the attribution of permanent establishment profits) |

The version currently in force is the **agreement of 18 February 2011** with the amendments of the protocol of 19 February 2021.

## Which types of income does the treaty cover?

### Art. 6 — Income from immovable property (real estate)

**Rule:** Income from real estate may be taxed in the **state where the property is located**.

**In practice for emigrants:**
- Rental income from a flat in Munich → taxed by **Germany**
- Rental income from a flat in Limassol → taxed by **Cyprus**
- If you are resident in Cyprus, Art. 22(3) provides for a credit for German tax paid in accordance with the treaty, limited to the Cypriot tax on that income.

> **Note:** Gains from the sale of real estate also fall to the state where the property is located. The German speculation period (10 years, § 23 EStG) continues to apply to German property.

### Art. 7 — Business profits

**Rule:** The profits of a company are taxed only in the **state of residence** — unless the company has a **permanent establishment** in the other state.

**What does that mean?**
- Your Cyprus Ltd makes profits → only Cyprus taxes them (corporate income tax 15%, since 2026)
- If your Cyprus Ltd has an office in Germany → the profits attributable to the German office are taxed in Germany
- Your German GmbH remains subject to tax in Germany, regardless of where you live

> **Important:** "Permanent establishment" is strictly defined (Art. 5 of the treaty): a fixed place of business, office, workshop, construction site (>12 months). A home office or management in Germany may or may not create a permanent establishment or German residence. That depends on the actual circumstances. The shareholder's place of residence alone does not decide it.

### Art. 10 — Dividends

**Rule:** Dividends may be taxed in the **state of residence** of the recipient. The source state (where the paying company is based) may, however, levy a **limited withholding tax**.

| Situation | Maximum withholding tax |
|---------------|----------------------|
| A company that is the beneficial owner and directly holds ≥10% of the capital | **5%** |
| All other cases | **15%** |

**Example:** You live in Cyprus and receive dividends from your German GmbH:
- Germany levies at most **15% withholding tax** (instead of 26.4% under national law)
- In Cyprus: with [non-dom status](/en/business/non-dom-status-cyprus-60-day-rule/), dividends are **exempt from SDC** (0%)
- A credit in Cyprus is limited to the Cypriot tax on the same income; at 0% SDC this gives no SDC refund. German relief has to be applied for separately

> **Note:** For individuals, the [BZSt refund procedure](https://online.portal.bzst.de/SharedDocs/Leistungsbeschreibung/DE/erstattung_abzugsteuer_kapitalertraege_50c_estg.html?nn=25056) should generally be examined. The exemption certificate for capital income is tied to special conditions for corporations; it is not generally available to a private shareholder.

### Art. 11 — Interest

**Rule:** Under Art. 11(1), interest that arises in one state may be taxed **only in the state of residence** if its beneficial owner is resident in the other state. Exceptions apply in particular where a permanent establishment is involved, and for amounts above an arm's-length level.

**In practice:** If a beneficial owner resident in Cyprus receives German interest covered by Art. 11, the treaty does not provide for a 10% German withholding tax. You must prove the relief under the applicable procedure. In Cyprus, SDC can fall away if the non-dom conditions are met. Check other levies separately.

### Art. 12 — Royalties

**Rule:** If the beneficial owner is resident in the other state, Art. 12 in principle gives the right to tax to the state of residence. Cases linked to a permanent establishment, and amounts above an arm's-length level, are regulated separately.

**What it means:** If you license rights from Cyprus to German companies (software, patents, trademarks), only Cyprus taxes them. Check German relief from withholding tax under the applicable BZSt procedure. For the Cyprus Ltd: 15% corporate tax on profit, for fully qualifying IP profits possibly an **effective 3%** from 2026: 80% deduction on qualifying profits × 15% corporate tax on the remaining share. [Income Tax Law, §9(1)(κ) and Second Schedule](<https://www.cylaw.org/nomoi/enop/non-ind/2002_1_118/full.html>).

### Art. 13 — Capital gains

| Asset | Right to tax |
|-----------|-------------------|
| Real estate | State where the property is located |
| Movable property of a permanent establishment | State of the permanent establishment |
| Ships/aircraft | Place of effective management |
| Shares that derive their value mainly from real estate | State where the property is located |
| All other assets | State of residence |

**For GmbH shares:** After you move away, the gain from selling your GmbH shares is in principle taxed in the **state of residence** (Cyprus). Cyprus levies **no capital gains tax** on gains from the disposal of shares in foreign companies. But: the [exit tax](/en/business/gmbh-exit-tax-planning/) (§ 6 AStG) already applies when you leave, before you become resident in Cyprus.

### Art. 14 — Income from employment (salary)

**Basic rule:** Salaries are taxed in the **state where the work is performed** — that is, where the work is physically carried out.

**Exception (183-day rule):** The remuneration may still be taxed only in the state of residence if:
1. The employee is present in the state of work for **no more than 183 days** in the 12-month period
2. The remuneration is paid by an employer who is **not** resident in the state of work
3. The remuneration is **not** borne by a permanent establishment in the state of work

**Practical cases:**

| Situation | Taxation |
|-----------|-------------|
| Remote work from Cyprus for a German employer | **Cyprus** (work is performed in Cyprus) |
| Business trip to Germany (under 183 days) | **Cyprus** (if all 3 conditions are met) |
| Secondment to Germany (over 183 days) | **Germany** for the work performed there |

> **Important for remote workers:** If you live in Cyprus and work from there for a German company, your salary is in principle taxable in Cyprus. The German employer must, however, check whether there is a payroll tax obligation in Cyprus.

### Art. 17 — Pensions and annuities

**Rule:** Private pensions and annuities are in principle taxed in the **state of residence**.

**Statutory German pension (DRV):** Art. 17(2) allows Germany to tax payments from its statutory social insurance. A DRV pension is therefore not automatically taxable exclusively in Cyprus. If you are resident in Cyprus, the credit rules of Art. 22(3) apply.

For foreign pensions that qualify accordingly, the current Cypriot [Income Tax Law §20](https://www.cylaw.org/nomoi/enop/ind/2002_1_118/section-sc16595bc9-1364-e637-ece1-50318dea73a0.html) allows an annual choice between the normal rate and 5% on the amount above €5,000. This Cypriot election does not remove a German right to tax.

### Art. 18 — Public service

**Rule (paying-state principle):** Remuneration and pensions for services rendered to a contracting state are taxed in the **paying state**.

**In practice:**
- Civil servant pensions (federal, state, municipal) → taxed by **Germany**, even if you live in Cyprus
- Bundeswehr (armed forces) pensions → **Germany**
- Exception: if the recipient is a national of the state of residence (that is, a Cypriot) and resident there → then the state of residence taxes

> **Note:** German civil servant pensions are in principle taxed in Germany. However, if the recipient is resident in Cyprus and a Cypriot national, Art. 18(2)(b) assigns the right to tax to Cyprus. Other income (e.g. a private pension, rental income in Cyprus) is taxed under the treaty rules.

## What is the progression clause?

Even where the treaty **exempts** income from German taxation, that income is taken into account to calculate the German tax rate (§ 32b EStG).

**Example:** In the year you move, you earn €30,000 in salary in Germany until June and €30,000 in Cyprus from July. The Cypriot €30,000 is not taxed. But the rate on the German €30,000 is calculated as if you had earned €60,000 → higher average tax rate.

**When is the progression clause relevant?**
- In the **year of the move** (partial income in both countries)
- In the case of **limited tax liability with an application under § 1(3) EStG** (if you choose to be taxed as if unlimited)
- With **rental income in Germany** and simultaneous exempt foreign income

## How does the treaty work in practice?

### Retiree in Paphos with a DRV pension

A DRV pension of €18,000 is not tax-free worldwide merely because of the Cypriot allowance. Germany may tax it under Art. 17(2); the Cypriot calculation and any credit for German tax must be checked separately.

### Remote worker in Limassol for a German employer

- Salary: €72,000/year
- Under treaty Art. 14: taxable in Cyprus (the work is performed there)
- Cypriot income tax depends on the tax year, permitted deductions and, where applicable, an employment exemption
- German tax consequences have to be checked based on residence and any work performed in Germany
- Residence and remaining German tax obligations: [clarification with the tax office (Finanzamt)](/en/life/finanzamt-after-move/)

### GmbH owner with dividends

- Dividend from a German GmbH: €100,000
- Withholding tax in Germany: max. 15% (treaty Art. 10) = **€15,000**
- In Cyprus with non-dom status: 0% SDC on dividends
- German withholding tax in the example: **15%** if the treaty conditions are met. Other burdens such as GESY and the corporate tax already paid by the GmbH have to be considered separately
- As an individual shareholder, check the BZSt refund procedure for excess withholding tax (Kapitalertragsteuer)

## How do I claim treaty benefits?

### 1. Residence certificate from Cyprus

Apply for the **Tax Residency Certificate** from the Cypriot Tax Department. Prerequisite: tax residence (183-day rule or [60-day rule](/en/business/non-dom-status-cyprus-60-day-rule/)).

### 2. Relief procedure in Germany

For individuals, a refund of excess withholding tax (Kapitalertragsteuer) on German dividends from the BZSt generally comes into consideration. Proof of residence and of the tax withheld is required. An exemption certificate under §50c(2) no. 1 EStG requires a qualifying corporation with at least 10% direct shareholding; under the [BZSt procedure](https://online.portal.bzst.de/SharedDocs/Leistungsbeschreibung/DE/freistellung_abzugsteuer_kapitalertraege.html?nn=23996) its validity is at least one and at most five years.

### 3. Inform the tax office (Finanzamt)

- Notify your new address
- Point out the application of the treaty
- In the case of limited tax liability: check, where applicable, an application under § 1(3) EStG (it can bring back the basic allowance, but triggers the progression clause)

## What mistakes are common?

1. **No residence certificate applied for** — without it the German tax office does not accept the treaty exemption
2. **Dual residence** — if you keep your German home, you remain subject to tax in Germany. The treaty has tie-breaker rules (Art. 4), but the situation is complex
3. **Permanent establishment overlooked** — regular business activity from Germany can create a permanent establishment
4. **Paying-state principle ignored** — civil servant pensions are in principle taxed in Germany; the exception for Cypriot nationals resident in Cyprus under Art. 18(2)(b) must be checked
5. **Progression clause forgotten** — exempt income raises the tax rate on other German income
6. **Relief procedures confused** — for dividends, individuals must in principle check the refund procedure; an exemption certificate is not open to every recipient

## Frequently asked questions (FAQ)

**Do I need a tax adviser for the double tax treaty?**
Strongly recommended. The treaty has nuances (progression clause, permanent establishment, tie-breaker rules) that cause mistakes without expert knowledge. Ideally: an adviser who knows both German and Cypriot tax law.

**Can I work from Cyprus as a remote worker for a German employer without paying tax in Germany?**
In principle yes. Under treaty Art. 14, salary is taxed in the state where the work is performed — if you work in Cyprus, Cyprus taxes it. What matters is your treaty residence, the physical place of work and the relevant exceptions. De-registering your address in Germany does not by itself decide tax liability. If you have working days in Germany, all conditions of the exception must be checked; the length of stay alone is not enough.

**How long does the residence certificate from Cyprus take?**
A general processing time has not been verified here; ask the competent Tax Department for the current status. Prerequisite: tax residence (183-day rule or 60-day rule) and registration with the Tax Department.

**Are civil servant pensions also taxed in Cyprus?**
In principle Germany taxes them. Civil servant pensions fall under the paying-state principle (treaty Art. 18) and are in principle taxed in Germany. Exception: residence and citizenship in Cyprus under Art. 18(2)(b). Other income (e.g. a private pension) follows the normal treaty rules.
