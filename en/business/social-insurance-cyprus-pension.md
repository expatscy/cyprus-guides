Social Insurance covers insured employees and self-employed people under different rules. Paying contributions does not automatically qualify a person for every benefit: insurance history, employment category and the conditions of the particular benefit matter. [Social Insurance Law](https://www.cylaw.org/nomoi/enop/non-ind/2010_1_59/full.html).

## What are the contribution rates?

### Employees

| Payer | Share of insurable earnings |
|-------|----------------------------|
| Employee | 8.8% |
| Employer | 8.8% |
| State | 5.2% |
| **Total Social Insurance funding** | **22.8%** |

These are the applied rates in force from 1 January 2024. They describe Social Insurance funding, rather than every deduction or employer levy. [Official contribution rates](https://www.gov.cy/mlsi-eurescy/en/documents/apply-for-a-social-insurance-number/).

For **2026**, the employee insurable-earnings ceiling is **EUR 5,742 per month** for monthly-paid employees, effective 1 January, or **EUR 1,325 per week** for weekly-paid employees, effective 5 January. These ceilings do not establish a GESY ceiling. [Official 2026 ceiling notice](https://www.mlsi.gov.cy/mlsi/sid/sidv2.nsf/All/8477F2B7998AD8F1C2258D6800300FC5).

### Self-employed people

The self-employed person's Social Insurance contribution is **16.6%** of the applicable insurable earnings. The 2026 occupational table sets weekly minimum earnings by occupation and, where specified, experience; its maximum is **EUR 1,325 per week**. It applies from **5 January 2026 to 3 January 2027**. This is a different basis from an employee's monthly salary. [Official 2026 occupational table](https://www.mlsi.gov.cy/mlsi/sid/sidv2.nsf/All/E3FAA841210065C3C2258E37002C0420?OpenDocument).

The official 2026 contribution table also specifies **GESY at 4%** and an **HRDA contribution of 0.5% from 5 January 2026** on weekly insurable earnings. GESY has separate assessment rules; do not assume that the Social Insurance maximum caps every charge. [Official self-employed contribution table](https://www.mlsi.gov.cy/mlsi/sid/sidv2.nsf/All/CC73852B37301B20C2258E37002C21E4?OpenDocument). See the [self-employment guide](/en/business/self-employed-cyprus-guide/) for registration, occupational categories and applications to use actual income.

## How do I get a contribution statement?

Use the [official total-contribution statement service](https://www.gov.cy/en/service/social-insurance-total-contribution-statement/) with an **authenticated CY Login account**. You can obtain a statement for a particular year or for the full record, for personal or official use.

The service's [FAQ](https://sis-citizen-reporting.service.gov.cy/en/faq) explains the record's **insurable and assimilated earnings and insurance units**. It distinguishes actual earnings and actual GHS earnings. Amounts from 2008 onwards are displayed in euros. Missing entries can have several causes, including records still being finalised or technical issues; ask Social Insurance Services to investigate before concluding that contributions were not paid.

The official service page lists district contacts, including **veveosislim@sid.mlsi.gov.cy** for Limassol. Confirm the required identity checks and delivery method with the service rather than sending identity documents based on an informal template. Its **1450 support line operates Monday to Friday, 08:00–17:00**; this is a support-line schedule, not an office-opening schedule.

## How do I register as self-employed?

The [official self-employed insurance service](https://www.gov.cy/service/asfalisi-aftotelos-ergazomenon/) provides registration form **YKA 1-013**, district contact details and submission instructions. It also provides separate forms for occupation changes, termination, periods of non-employment and an application to pay on actual income.

Check your immigration route's right to self-employment separately. Social Insurance registration does not itself grant permission to work. See the [self-employment guide](/en/business/self-employed-cyprus-guide/) before choosing the appropriate form and evidence.

## How does the pension work?

The ordinary statutory pension age is **65**. Pension from **63** is possible only under the specific early-pension conditions, including the required insurance record or qualifying invalidity route; it is not an option available automatically to everyone. [Official pension guidance](https://www.mlsi.gov.cy/mlsi/sdg/sdg.nsf/All/4678B9CAD7F09671C2258DA20047FD79).

The ordinary insurance conditions include:

- At least **15 actual basic-insurance units** and **780 weeks elapsed since insurance began**.
- Actual and assimilated basic-insurance units amounting to at least **30% of the years in the statutory reference period**.

These are insurance-unit and elapsed-time tests, rather than a guarantee of a full pension after 15 working years. The pension has basic and supplementary components calculated under the statutory rules. Early commencement under the reduction rules reduces the pension by **0.5% for each whole or part month before age 65**, reaching **12% when it starts at 63**; this reduction continues for life. [Social Insurance Law, section 35, section 35A and Third Schedule](https://www.cylaw.org/nomoi/enop/non-ind/2010_1_59/full.html).

### Insurance in other countries

The official pension guidance describes aggregation of **EU, EEA and Swiss** periods when needed to meet qualifying conditions. Cyprus generally requires **at least one year of Cyprus insurance** for its pension under these coordination rules, and calculates a proportional pension for the Cyprus periods. Aggregation does not move pension funds into Cyprus or guarantee entitlement regardless of the remaining conditions. Keep your records and request a case-specific assessment before retiring or moving abroad.

### UK pensions

A UK State Pension paid in Cyprus receives the **annual increase** under the UK rules for EEA countries. [GOV.UK pension uprating guidance](https://www.gov.uk/state-pension-if-you-retire-abroad/rates-of-state-pension).

Voluntary UK National Insurance contributions are subject to eligibility checks. For **2026–27 onwards**, voluntary Class 2 contributions cannot be paid for time abroad. Class 3 applications for time abroad after **5 April 2026** generally require **10 consecutive years of previous UK residence or 10 years of qualifying contributions**, with defined contribution categories and transitional exceptions for earlier applicants. National Insurance credits and other voluntary contributions paid for periods abroad do not count toward that 10-year contribution test. Check whether a payment would improve your pension before paying. [Current GOV.UK voluntary-contribution rules](https://www.gov.uk/voluntary-national-insurance-contributions/if-you-live-or-work-abroad).

For UK–Cyprus aggregation, ask the competent authorities which coordination framework applies to your personal history. Do not assume that one bilateral agreement covers every post-Brexit case.

## How do maternity, parental leave and sickness benefits work?

### Maternity benefit

Maternity **benefit** and an employee's statutory **leave** are distinct. The ordinary childbirth benefit runs for **22 consecutive weeks**, with an additional **four weeks for the third and subsequent childbirths**. Its starting week can be selected from the **ninth through the second week before the expected delivery week**. Special circumstances, including multiple births, have additional rules. [Social Insurance Law, section 29](https://www.cylaw.org/nomoi/enop/non-ind/2010_1_59/full.html).

Eligibility includes the required insurance record. The benefit is **72% of the basic and supplementary insured-earnings components** for the relevant contribution year. Only the basic component rises to **80%, 90% or 100% for one, two or three dependants** when the applicant is the sole household provider. Full employer pay excludes benefit for the same period; partial pay limits the combined payment to full pay. [Official maternity FAQ](https://maternity-benefit.service.gov.cy/en/faq). See the [birth guide](/en/life/birth-registration-cyprus/) for the insurance tests and application conditions.

### Parental leave

For employees, the ordinary parental-leave entitlement is **18 weeks per child**, after **six continuous months with the same employer**, for a child up to **15**. Leave is exercised in blocks of at least **one day**, with a maximum of **five weeks per calendar year**. Normally give **three weeks’ notice**; in an emergency, a shorter notice period requires employer agreement. Special rules extend the age limit for disability and the leave duration for widowhood or specified single-parent circumstances. Parental-leave **allowance has separate eligibility conditions**; not every week of leave is automatically paid. [Department of Labour Relations](https://www.mlsi.gov.cy/mlsi/dlr/dlr.nsf/parentalleave_en/parentalleave_en?Click=&OpenDocument=).

### Sickness benefit

The statutory Social Insurance waiting period is normally the **first three days**. For self-employed insurance under section 11, the normal sickness waiting period is **nine days**, with exceptions for an accident or at least one hospital overnight stay during those first nine days. Benefit also requires the relevant insurance and incapacity conditions. These waiting periods do not determine whether an employer pays wages under the employment arrangements. [Social Insurance Law, section 31](https://www.cylaw.org/nomoi/enop/non-ind/2010_1_59/full.html).

## What is the redundancy fund?

The **Redundancy Fund contribution is 1.2%, paid entirely by the employer**. It is separate from the employee's Social Insurance contribution. A payment from the fund requires assessment of the applicable redundancy conditions; dismissal alone does not guarantee compensation. [Official employer-contribution guidance](https://www.mlsi.gov.cy/mlsi/sdg/sdg.nsf/All/EA4F396F80C4BAA8C22586A10042659F).

## What practical steps should I take?

1. Save contribution statements and relevant correspondence.
2. Compare the official record with your payslips and ask about discrepancies.
3. Use the correct self-employed registration or change-of-status form.
4. Request a pension assessment based on insurance units and any foreign periods.
5. Check the current official rates and contact arrangements before paying or visiting an office.
