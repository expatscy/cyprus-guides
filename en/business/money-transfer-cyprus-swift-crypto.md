> **Short answer:** The cheapest way to send money internationally from Cyprus is Wise (0.4-0.6% fee, 1-2 days). SWIFT transfers cost EUR 15-45 per transaction and take 2-5 business days.


Transferring money to Cyprus is one of the main headaches for expats. Whether you need to fund a [bank account](/en/business/bank-account-cyprus-foreigners/) or pay for [property](/en/property/buying-property-cyprus/), this guide covers all available methods. If you are transferring specifically from Russia, see our dedicated [Russia-to-Cyprus transfer guide](/en/business/money-transfer-russia-cyprus/) for sanctions workarounds.

> **Official sources:**
> - [Central Bank of Cyprus](https://www.centralbank.cy/) — Central Bank
> - [CySEC](https://www.cysec.gov.cy/) — Securities Commission (crypto exchanges)
> - [Wise](https://wise.com/) / [Revolut](https://www.revolut.com/) — Popular transfer services

## What is the big picture?

### The Problem

Cyprus banks **very thoroughly** check incoming payments:

- **Proof of source of funds** required
- Any "cryptocurrency trace" — **red flag**
- SWIFT from Russia — under close scrutiny

### The Main Rule

> "If you want to bring clean money to your local account — step one: go to your banker and ask if you can send, what justification is needed and what documents."

## How do SWIFT transfers work?

### From Where It Works

- **Kazakhstan, Georgia, Armenia** — relatively stable
- **Russia** — difficult, but possible through some banks
- **OTP (Hungary)** — there's experience with transfers

### What the Bank Needs

For receiving SWIFT, the Cyprus bank will request:

- **Source of funds** (where the money came from)
- **Documents** (income certificates, property sale, etc.)
- **Pre-approval** — especially for amounts from €10,000

### Important Nuance

> "Usually when Bank of Cyprus receives money it wasn't expecting, it holds it without crediting until the owner provides compliance documents. This money isn't visible even in pending — only to the manager."

### Amounts Over €24,000

- Automatically go through **compliance**
- Without documents — **returned to sender**
- Account **may be blocked**

## How does cryptocurrency work for transfers?

### Banks' Position

⚠️ **Bank of Cyprus and Eurobank Limited (formerly Hellenic Bank) don't like cryptocurrency**

> "Money from crypto Bank of Cyprus usually doesn't accept and closes the account."

### What This Means

- Transfer from a **crypto exchange** to bank — high risk
- **P2P transfers** — even higher risk
- Even if the money is "clean" — bank may refuse

### Revolut and Crypto

A separate topic — **Revolut** and cryptocurrency:

> "Don't transfer crypto to Revolut from outside. They don't like this, lots of blocks."

**Especially dangerous:**

- Crypto via **P2P** — frequent blocks
- Crypto from **unknown wallets**

**Less risky:**

- Crypto through **licensed exchanges** (but still risk)

### Cash Withdrawal

Cryptocurrency **can** be used in Cyprus:

- Withdrawal to **cash** through exchangers
- Payment with crypto exchange **cards**
- **Don't deposit** to Cyprus bank account

## Can I use Revolut and Wise in Cyprus?

### What They're Good For

- **Everyday expenses**
- **Receiving payments** from foreign companies
- **Alternative** to Cyprus bank

### Limitations

**Revolut** requires:

- **[Tax identification number](/en/business/tax-identification-number-cyprus/)** (TIN) of country of residence
- **Proof of address** (ARC or utility bill)
- Periodic **document checks**

### Blocks

Revolut **may block** account for:

- Expired documents (ARC)
- Suspicious transactions
- No response to requests

> "Revolut blocked account for expired documents. Papers weren't reviewed, upload window disappeared."

### Revolut Pro

For **regular payments** from employers:

> "Revolut Pro is more lenient about such payments. But still discuss with support."

## What practical scenarios should I consider?

### Scenario 1: Selling Real Estate in Russia

**Community question:**

> "I'm selling property in Russia, want to transfer to Cyprus for purchase here. How?"

**Options:**

1. **Through a third country** (Kazakhstan, Georgia) — open account, transfer, then to Cyprus
2. **Crypto** — risky for bank account
3. **Cash** — there are import limits

**Key:** Coordinate with Cyprus bank **in advance**, show sale documents.

### Scenario 2: Remote Work for Crypto

**Question:**

> "I earn €10,000/month in crypto. How do I deposit to Cyprus bank?"

**Honest answer:**

- To Cyprus bank — **very difficult**
- Use crypto exchange **cards** for purchases
- **Revolut/Wise** — with caution
- **Cash** — for large purchases

### Scenario 3: Salary from Abroad

**Situation:** Working for a UK company, receiving in Cyprus.

**What's needed:**

- **Contract** with the company
- **Invoices** (if freelancer)
- Declare income, pay [taxes](/en/business/taxes-cyprus-gesy-deductions/)

**Where to receive:**

- **Cyprus bank** — if you have a contract and everything is official
- **Revolut/Wise** — easier, but risk of blocks

## What transfer methods definitely do not work?

### Direct Transfer from Crypto Exchange to Bank

> "€24k arrived — automatic compliance. If there's crypto trace — 100% return and possible block."

### Savings Without Documents

> "If savings are >€10-20k without confirmations and ability to trace transactions — chances are negligible."

### SWIFT from Sanctioned Banks

Doesn't work from banks under sanctions.

## What do experts recommend?

1. **Coordinate with bank in advance** — especially amounts from €5,000
2. **Keep all documents** — source of funds, contracts, certificates
3. **Don't mix crypto and bank** — these are different worlds in Cyprus
4. **Have a backup channel** — Revolut/Wise in case of blocks
5. **Consult your banker** — each bank has its own rules

Transferring money to Cyprus requires preparation and documents. The main thing is transparency of source of funds and advance coordination with the bank.
