> **In short:** After the 19th sanctions package, MIR and SBP are blocked. What still works: SWIFT through non-sanctioned banks, Profee, KwikPay, P2P cryptocurrency, and transfers through third countries.

This is the updated guide for 2026. On 25 January 2026 the EU's 19th sanctions package came into force, blocking the MIR system and SBP for cross-border transfers. There are fewer working methods now, and fees have gone up. Here is what works today. If you are only preparing to [move to Cyprus](/en/immigration/moving-to-cyprus-complete-guide/), open an account at a non-sanctioned bank before you leave (see the [checklist for leaving Russia](/en/life/leaving-russia-tax-residency/)).

## What changed in 2026?

### The EU's 19th sanctions package (25 January 2026)

- **MIR system** — blocked for cross-border transactions with the EU
- **SBP (Faster Payments System)** — likewise banned for cross-border transactions
- Consequence: a number of services that used MIR/SBP to pay out to Russian cards stopped working

### What stopped working earlier

- **KoronaPay (Zolotaya Korona)** — KoronaPay Europe has stopped new transfers and is giving up its Central Bank of Cyprus licence ([koronapay.com](https://koronapay.com/transfers/europe/), checked 7 November 2026)
- **Paysend** — stopped serving the Cyprus → Russia route
- **Gazprombank** — was added to the US SDN list (November 2024). Its status in the EU is ambiguous, but Cypriot banks have stopped accepting transfers from Gazprombank

## How does SWIFT through non-sanctioned banks work?

### How it works

SWIFT transfers are formally available through Russian banks that remain connected to the system:

- **Raiffeisenbank Russia**
- **UniCredit Bank Russia**
- **OTP Bank Russia**
- Others: Renaissance Credit, Asia-Pacific Bank, Credit Europe Bank, BKS Bank, BBR Bank

### Route

1. Transfer roubles to an account at Raiffeisenbank/UniCredit/OTP in Russia
2. Convert to EUR
3. SWIFT transfer to your Cypriot account

### Reality

| Parameter | Details |
|----------|--------|
| Fee | 1.5–5% (depends on the bank and the amount). In isolated cases up to 15–50% at small banks |
| Speed | 3–14 business days |
| Reliability | Medium. A transfer can be blocked by a correspondent bank |
| Limits | Depend on the bank. OTP: from $500, no upper limit (but compliance checks amounts over $10,000) |

> **Important:** SWIFT transfers from Russia to the EU pass through correspondent banks (usually Deutsche Bank, Commerzbank, JP Morgan). Any of them can block a transfer at the screening stage. This is not rare: by some estimates around 30–40% of transfers via OTP Bank Russia are rejected. When a transfer is blocked the money is returned, but it takes 2–4 weeks.

### Preparation

- Open an account at a non-sanctioned bank in advance (before moving or during a visit to Russia)
- Prepare the **payment purpose** in English: "Transfer of own funds" or "Personal savings transfer"
- It is better to split the amount: several transfers of €5,000–10,000 are more reliable than one large one
- Keep all payment orders — your Cypriot bank will ask for them

## How does Profee work?

### How it works

[Profee](https://www.profee.com/) is a money transfer service (registered in the EU). It lets you send money from Europe to Russia; its site says transfers to some Russian banks are restricted by sanctions.

### Route

1. Funds from your Cypriot account or European card → Profee
2. Profee → conversion → credited to a Russian account (or the other way round)

### Reality

| Parameter | Details |
|----------|--------|
| Fee | Advertised as 0%, but the exchange rate includes a 1–3% margin |
| Speed | 1–3 business days |
| Limits | Depend on verification. Usually up to €5,000 per transaction |
| Directions | Europe → Russia and Russia → Europe |

> **Important:** Profee is NOT a transfer of your own funds. The Cypriot bank sees a payment from a legal entity (Profee), not a self-to-self transfer. For large amounts the bank may ask for an explanation. From an AML point of view this is a less "clean" route than a direct SWIFT transfer.

### Limitations

- Availability for Russian citizens may change — check the website before using it
- Not all Russian banks accept incoming transfers from Profee
- For mortgage payments and regular transfers it works, but with caveats

## How does KwikPay work?

### How it works

[KwikPay](https://kwikpay.com/) is a money transfer service with physical offices in Cyprus and Greece. It works with 20+ countries, including Russia.

### Route

1. Go to a KwikPay office in Cyprus in person
2. Pay in cash or transfer from an account
3. The recipient in Russia gets cash or a credit to their account

### Reality

| Parameter | Details |
|----------|--------|
| Fee | 0–5% (depends on the amount and direction) |
| Limit | Up to €3,000 per transaction |
| Speed | Same day |
| Requirement | In-person visit to the office |

### Who it suits

- Regular small transfers (helping relatives, paying utility bills)
- If you do not want to route transfers through a bank account
- The €3,000 limit is a constraint: it does not suit large amounts

## How do you transfer money with cryptocurrency?

### How it works

P2P platforms (Bybit, OKX and others) let you buy and sell crypto (usually USDT) directly between individuals.

### Route (Russia → Cyprus)

1. Buy USDT for roubles via P2P (transfer to the seller's card in Russia)
2. USDT in your crypto wallet
3. Sell USDT for EUR on P2P (credited to a European account)

### Route (Cyprus → Russia)

1. Buy USDT for EUR via P2P
2. Sell USDT for roubles (the seller transfers to your card in Russia)

### Reality

| Parameter | Details |
|----------|--------|
| Fee | 1–3% (P2P sellers' margin + spread) |
| Speed | From minutes to a few hours |
| Limits | Depend on the P2P seller. Usually from $100 to $50,000 |
| Risk | Medium–high |

### Risks

- **Bank account closure.** Cypriot banks close accounts when they detect crypto transactions without documentation. This is a real risk, not isolated cases
- **Crypto account closure.** In September–November 2025 Bybit began closing accounts of Russian citizens with EU residency. The status keeps changing — check before using
- **Volatility.** USDT is pegged to the dollar, but with RUB → USDT → EUR conversion you pass through an exchange rate twice
- **Fraud.** There are scammers on P2P platforms. Use only verified sellers with a high rating

> **Important:** If you use crypto for transfers and then deposit EUR into a Cypriot account, the bank may ask about the origin of the funds. Prepare screenshots of the P2P deals and your transaction history on the exchange. Without documentation, expect the account to be blocked.

## How do you transfer through third countries?

### Kazakhstan

- **Opening an account in person:** since January 2025, remote opening for non-residents outside the state programme has been closed. Banks: Freedom Finance, Halyk Bank, Kaspi
- **e-Residency (NEW, 2026):** state programme [eresidency.gov.kz](https://eresidency.gov.kz/en) — digital residency for ages 18+, open to any nationality (no citizenship restrictions). It costs $499/year (promo) or $999/year, payable by card or USDT/USDC. It gives you an IIN, a digital ID card and an eSIM. Remote account opening with partners: Freedom Bank, RBK Bank. BCC and Bereke Bank were announced for January 2026 — check the current status. The bank runs its own KYC, so there is no guarantee
- Route: Russia → Kazakhstan (rouble transfer) → conversion to EUR → SWIFT to Cyprus (it also works in reverse: Cyprus → Kazakhstan → Russia)
- MIR card support: until recently it worked; after the 19th package the status is ambiguous

### Armenia

**Works in both directions:** Armenian banks are one of the few channels where the route "Russia → Armenia → Cyprus" **and** "Cyprus → Armenia → Russia" work symmetrically. You can go either way, depending on which direction you need the money to move today. The banks have rouble accounts and direct correspondent relationships with Russia, plus the usual European SWIFT corridor.

- **Banks with rouble SWIFT to/from Russia:** Ameriabank, Acba, Ardshinbank, Inecobank
- **Opening an account:** usually in person at a branch (Yerevan, 1–2 hours). Evocabank previously supported instant transfers from T-Bank — the channel was temporarily suspended in 2025, check the current status
- **Route Russia → Cyprus:** roubles → SWIFT to an Armenian bank → conversion to EUR → SWIFT to Cyprus
- **Route Cyprus → Russia:** EUR from a Cypriot bank → Armenian account → conversion to roubles → transfer to a Russian card
- Fee: 1–3% per leg (with double conversion — up to 5% in total)

### Kyrgyzstan (MBank, Bakai Bank)

- Remote opening via a notarised power of attorney + a representative in Bishkek
- Intermediaries: $50–150 (EasyCrds, Hill Consulting)
- VISA card, multi-currency account, SWIFT

> **Important:** MBank states that a personal visit is mandatory. Intermediaries offer opening by power of attorney — this is a grey area, with no guarantees.

## What are the limits and compliance requirements?

### Russian side

- **Foreign-currency transfers from Russia by individuals**: under Law 173-FZ there are no restrictions on transfers of your own funds (for non-sanctioned banks)
- **Rosfinmonitoring control**: transactions of 600,000 ₽ or more (~€5,500) are subject to mandatory control (Law 522-FZ). The review period is 10 days (not business days)
- **Structuring** — deliberately splitting into amounts below 600,000 ₽ to avoid control — is itself a suspicious transaction

### Cypriot side

- **Incoming transfers >€15,000**: the bank must check the source (4AMLD/6AMLD)
- **Cash of €10,000 or more**: customs declaration on entry/exit
- **Regular receipts without a contract**: if you receive transfers every month but have no employment contract or invoices, the bank will ask

> **Practical tip:** The best size for a single transfer is €3,000–10,000. Large enough not to overpay for lots of transactions, but not so large that it triggers an automatic check. Transfer regularly (once or twice a month), not erratically.

## What should you definitely not do?

1. **Deposit large amounts of crypto into a bank account without documents** — the account will be closed
2. **Use unknown intermediaries** (Telegram channels "I transfer money, 2% fee") — fraud and/or money laundering
3. **Split transfers artificially** — banks see patterns, and so does Rosfinmonitoring
4. **Lie to the bank about the origin of funds** — criminal liability under the laws of Cyprus and the EU
5. **Send large sums through friends or acquaintances** without paperwork — the recipient will have problems with the tax authority (yours and the Cypriot one)
6. **Use Pyypl** — numerous fraud complaints on Trustpilot

## What do we recommend?

1. **For regular transfers** (mortgage, helping family): SWIFT through Raiffeisenbank if you have an account. The alternative is KwikPay for small amounts
2. **For large amounts** (buying property, moving savings): SWIFT through a non-sanctioned bank. Split into several transfers. Prepare documents proving the source of funds
3. **For urgent transfers**: crypto P2P (with caution and documentation)
4. **Keep an account at a non-sanctioned bank** in Russia — it is your main channel for SWIFT
5. **Document everything** — every transfer, every conversion. The Cypriot bank will ask

> The situation with transfers changes every few months. Check that the information is current before large transactions. Consult a tax adviser on declaring your funds.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the most reliable way to transfer money from Russia to Cyprus in 2026?**
SWIFT through a non-sanctioned bank (Raiffeisenbank, UniCredit, OTP). The fee is 1.5–5% and it takes 3–14 days. According to user reports, a significant share of transfers via OTP is rejected by correspondent banks (unofficially around 30–40%, source: discussions on Banki.ru and specialised Telegram channels, [UNVERIFIED]). The money is returned, but keep copies of all payment orders: a refund takes 2–4 weeks.

**Does KoronaPay (Zolotaya Korona) work in 2026?**
No. KoronaPay Europe, the company licensed by the Central Bank of Cyprus, says new transfers are unavailable and that it is voluntarily giving up its licence ([koronapay.com](https://koronapay.com/transfers/europe/), checked 7 November 2026).

**Can I use cryptocurrency to transfer money?**
Technically yes, through P2P platforms (Bybit, OKX). The fee is 1–3% and it takes minutes. But the risk is high: Cypriot banks close accounts when they detect crypto transactions without documentation. More in the "Method 4" section above.

**What is the maximum transfer I can make without problems?**
The sweet spot is €3,000–10,000 at a time, once or twice a month. In Russia, transactions of 600,000 ₽ or more (~€5,500) are subject to Rosfinmonitoring control. In Cyprus, incoming transfers above €15,000 are checked by the bank.
