> **In short:** When you emigrate from Germany, exit tax on GmbH shares (a deemed disposal) can apply, from a 1% shareholding. Deferral is possible.

If you are a GmbH shareholder moving to Cyprus, you have to deal with the exit tax under § 6 AStG (Außensteuergesetz, the German Foreign Tax Act). This tax on a deemed capital gain is one of the most expensive pitfalls of emigrating, and the 2022 reform made it much stricter. Here is what to expect and what planning options exist.

## What is the exit tax?

If you are fully tax-liable in Germany and give up your residence or habitual abode, the tax office treats your shares in corporations (e.g. a GmbH) **as if you had sold them at market value**, even though you sold nothing. Income tax is due on the notional gain.

### When does § 6 AStG apply?

The exit tax is triggered if:

- In the **12 years** before you leave, you were fully tax-liable in Germany for at least **7 years**
- You hold shares in a corporation (GmbH, AG, UG etc.)
- At any time in the last **5 years** you held **at least 1%** of the company

> **Important:** The 1% threshold looks back 5 years. Even if you hold less than 1% when you leave, the tax applies if you were above the threshold at any point in the last 5 years.

### What is taxed?

The **notional capital gain** is taxed: market value of the shares on the day of departure minus acquisition cost. The gain is subject to regular income tax under the partial income method (§ 3 No. 40 EStG): only **60%** of the gain is taxable. With a top rate of 42% (from €66,760, 2026) or 45% (the "rich tax" rate from €277,826), the effective burden is **about 25–27%** of the increase in value.

**Worked example:**

| Item | Amount |
|----------|--------|
| Market value of the GmbH shares | €500,000 |
| Acquisition cost | €25,000 |
| Notional gain | €475,000 |
| Taxable portion (60%) | €285,000 |
| Income tax (approx. 42% + solidarity surcharge) | **approx. €126,000** |

## What changed in 2022?

With the ATAD Implementation Act of 25 June 2021 (in force since 1 January 2022), the rules became **much stricter**:

### Old rules (departure up to 31.12.2021)

- Move within the **EU/EEA**: **indefinite, interest-free deferral** without security (§ 6(5) AStG, old version)
- Cyprus is an EU member, so the tax was **deferred indefinitely**
- In practice: no payment as long as you stayed in another EU country

### New rules (departure from 01.01.2022)

- **No more indefinite deferral**, not even within the EU
- Instead: payment in **7 equal annual instalments** (§ 6(4) AStG, new version)
- Instalments on application, as a rule **only against security**
- The instalments are **interest-free**
- No more distinction between EU/EEA and third countries

> **Note:** For old cases (departure before 01.01.2022) with an existing EU deferral, the old law generally still applies (§ 21(3) AStG). **But:** since August 2023, substantial profit distributions can lead to revocation of the deferral even in old cases.

## Can the exit tax be reversed?

The tax can **lapse retroactively** if you become fully tax-liable in Germany again within **7 years** of leaving (§ 6(3) AStG), provided that:

- The shares have **not been sold or transferred** in the meantime
- No profit distributions were made whose total value exceeds **25%** of the value on the day of departure
- The shares did not pass into business assets

> **Important:** The 7-year period is a hard limit. If you return after 7 years and 1 day, you have no right to reversal.

## How does the tax treaty affect the exit tax?

The Germany–Cyprus tax treaty (new version of 18 February 2011, amending protocol of 19 February 2021) allocates taxing rights. For the exit tax there is a special situation:

- The treaty generally allocates capital gains on shares to the **state of residence**
- The exit tax, however, taxes a **notional** gain while you are still German-resident
- Because the tax is levied at the time of departure (when you are still resident in Germany), it is **not in conflict with the treaty**
- Cyprus generally does **not** tax gains on the sale of shares in foreign companies (no capital gains tax on shares)

This means the exit tax is a **purely German matter** that the treaty does not prevent.

## What planning options exist before you leave?

> **Note:** The following strategies are general considerations and do not replace individual tax advice. Every case is different. Get advice from a tax adviser who knows both German and Cypriot tax law.

### 1. Optimise the valuation

The market value on the day of departure determines the tax. Measures:

- **Plan the timing of the move**: after a poor financial year or before an expected contract
- **Profit distributions before leaving**: they reduce the company value (caution: dividend tax in Germany is about 26.4%)
- **Provisions and investments**: can lower the value temporarily

### 2. Reduce the shareholding below 1%

If you reduce your shareholding below 1% in time (e.g. by transferring shares to family members or a trustee), § 6 AStG does not apply. **But:** the tax office checks whether the transfer has real economic substance. Purely tax-driven arrangements are treated as abuse (§ 42 AO).

### 3. Holding structure

A holding company can make sense to:

- Bundle shareholdings
- Influence the valuation
- Structure future disposals in a tax-efficient way

**Timing is decisive**: a restructuring shortly before departure is viewed critically.

### 4. Prepare the instalment application

Because instalment payment in 7 annual instalments is granted on application:

- Apply **early** (with the tax return for the year of departure)
- Plan for **security** (bank guarantee, land charges etc.)
- Prepare a liquidity plan for 7 years

## What mistakes do GmbH owners often make?

1. **Planning too late**: calling the tax adviser only when the removal van arrives. Planning should start 12–24 months before departure.
2. **Ignoring the business valuation**: the tax office will ask for a valuation. Commission a report early.
3. **Assuming old cases are safe**: even existing EU deferrals can be revoked since 2023 if profits are distributed.
4. **Dual residence**: anyone who does not fully give up their residence in Germany (e.g. keeps a flat) remains fully tax-liable in both countries.
5. **Overestimating treaty protection**: the treaty does not protect against the exit tax, because it applies while you are still German-resident.

## What should I ask my tax adviser?

The exit tax is complex and depends on the individual case. Ask your tax adviser these questions:

1. What is the **current market value** of my GmbH shares? Which valuation method will the tax office use?
2. Is a **profit distribution before departure** worthwhile (dividend tax of about 26.4% vs. reducing the departure value)?
3. What **security** is needed for the instalment payment?
4. Does a **restructuring** (holding, share transfer) make economic sense?
5. How does the move affect the **ongoing taxation** of the GmbH (permanent establishment, managing director remuneration)?
6. What happens if I **return within the 7 years**?

## Conclusion

Since 2022 the exit tax is relevant for every GmbH shareholder, including when moving within the EU. The former indefinite deferral for EU moves no longer exists. It has been replaced by 7 annual instalments against security. Plan early, have the company valued, and work with a tax adviser who knows both sides.

## Frequently asked questions (FAQ)

**Do I have to pay the exit tax immediately when I move to Cyprus?**
No. Since 2022 you can apply to pay in 7 equal annual instalments (§ 6(4) AStG). Condition: security (e.g. a bank guarantee). The instalments are interest-free.

**Does the exit tax also apply to a UG (haftungsbeschränkt)?**
Yes. The UG is a form of GmbH. Any corporation in which you held at least 1% at any time in the last 5 years falls under § 6 AStG.

**What happens if I return to Germany within 7 years?**
On application, the tax is cancelled retroactively (§ 6(3) AStG), provided you have not sold the shares and have not taken excessive profit distributions (max. 25% of the value at departure).

**Does the Germany-Cyprus tax treaty protect against the exit tax?**
No. The exit tax applies while you are still German-resident. The treaty does not apply, because at the time of taxation you are still liable to tax in Germany.

**How is the value of my GmbH shares determined?**
The tax office uses the market value (gemeiner Wert) on the day of departure. Methods: capitalised earnings value, DCF or multiples. Commission an independent valuation.
