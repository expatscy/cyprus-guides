> **Short answer:** Registering a company in Cyprus takes 5-10 business days and costs EUR 500-3,000 including government fees. You need a registered office address, a secretary, and at least one director.


Registering a private limited company (Ltd) in Cyprus takes 5-10 business days. A private company has no statutory minimum share capital, although €1,000 nominal share capital is common in practice. You need at least 1 director and 1 shareholder — neither must be Cyprus residents, though tax residency implications apply. Corporate tax is 15% from 1 January 2026. VAT registration is mandatory when turnover exceeds €15,600/year; the standard VAT rate is 19%. Register at the [Registrar of Companies](https://www.companies.gov.cy/en/).

Annual maintenance costs: €2,000/year for a dormant company, €3,000-5,000+ for an active company. This covers mandatory annual audit, accounting, secretary/registered office costs, and filings; the old €350 annual company levy was abolished from 2024 onward. With Non-Dom status (17-year exemption for new tax residents), dividends are taxed at 0% SDC — effective total tax rate approximately 15% on distributed profits. For comparison, a UK Ltd on profits above £250,000 pays 25% CIT plus up to 39.35% dividend tax.

**BCS/work visa holders cannot be directors or shareholders of a Cyprus company.** The Registrar of Companies and Migration Department exchange data — violations discovered during permit renewal result in permit refusal. Alternatives: register under a spouse (not on BCS), use nominee directors, or operate through a foreign entity. If you want to run a company alongside employment, see our [dual income guide](/en/business/company-and-employment-cyprus-dual-income/) for the legal structure and tax implications.

## What are the main business forms?

### Limited Company (Private Company Limited by Shares)

The most common form — **private company limited by shares**:

- No statutory minimum share capital for a private company; **€1,000** nominal capital is common in practice
- Minimum **1 director** and **1 shareholder** required
- Director doesn't have to be a Cyprus resident (but there are tax implications)
- Registration takes **5-10 business days**

### Self-Employed

**Self-employed** status allows you to work as a sole proprietor:

- Suitable for freelancers and consultants
- Simplified reporting
- But for third-country nationals **special permission is required**

## What restrictions apply to BCS/work visa holders?

This is one of the most important nuances that many don't know about.

### You Cannot Register a Company in Your Name

If you work in Cyprus on a **BCS/high-skill visa**, then:

- **Cannot** be a director or shareholder of a Cyprus company
- **Cannot** provide services as self-employed on Cyprus territory
- If discovered, immigration may **refuse to extend your residence permit**

### How This Is Monitored

Based on community information:

- **Registrar of Companies** and **Migration Department** exchange data
- Checks occur during **residence permit submission/renewal**
- If a violation is found, you'll be asked to **transfer the company to someone else** or **give up your work visa**

### What to Do If You Need Your Own Business

Options for those who want to run a business but are on a work visa:

1. **Register the company under your spouse** (if they're not on a BCS visa)
2. **Use nominee directors** (but this has its own risks)
3. **Work through a foreign company** (outside Cyprus)
4. **Change your immigration status** to visitor residence permit

## What are the options for spouses of BCS employees?

For **spouses of BCS holders**, special rules apply:

### What You Can Do:

- Work as an employee through **Spouse Employment** (minimum €1,000/month)
- Get employed at **any Cyprus company**
- Open a company and **hire yourself as an employee** (with nuances)

### What You Cannot Do:

- Work as **self-employed**
- Provide services in Cyprus **without an employment contract**

### The Own Company Scheme

According to community discussions, a BCS spouse can:

1. Register a company
2. Hire themselves as an **employee** of that company
3. Obtain **Spouse Employment** permission
4. Pay themselves a salary (minimum €1,000)

**Important:** Being **only a director without salary** — not allowed. You need actual employment.

## How much does company maintenance cost?

### Minimum Annual Expenses:

| Company Status | Approximate Annual Cost |
|----------------|-------------------------|
| Dormant company (no operations) | from €2,000 |
| VAT-registered company | from €3,000 |
| Active company | from €3,000-5,000+ |

### What's Included:

- No current annual company levy from 2024 onward (old unpaid levies for 2011-2023 can still matter)
- **Audit** (mandatory for all companies)
- **Accounting services**
- **Registered office** (legal address)

### Salary Taxes:

With a minimum salary of €1,000/month, tax expenses will be approximately **€300/month**:

- Social Insurance
- GESY
- Redundancy Fund
- Other contributions

## How does VAT registration work?

VAT registration is mandatory if:

- Turnover exceeds **€15,600** per year
- You provide services to clients in the EU (B2B)

### Features:

- VAT rate — **19%**
- For B2B services in the EU, **reverse charge** often applies
- VAT-registered company — more complex and expensive reporting

**Community tip:** For small events and services, people often work through **self-employed without VAT** — this is more competitive.

## How does Cyprus company taxation work?

### Corporate Tax:

- **15%** on profit from 1 January 2026
- Dividends in Cyprus — **0%** for [Non-Dom individuals](/en/business/non-dom-status-cyprus-60-day-rule) (5% SDC if domiciled)
- Interest — special regime
- **IP Box** — effective **~3% tax** on qualifying intellectual property income

### UK Ltd vs Cyprus Ltd

For British entrepreneurs weighing their options:

| | UK Ltd | Cyprus Ltd |
|---|---|---|
| Corporation tax | 25% (profits over £250K) | **15%** |
| Small profits rate | 19% (up to £50K) | 15% (flat) |
| Dividend tax | 8.75–39.35% (on top of CIT) | **0%** with Non-Dom |
| VAT rate | 20% | 19% |
| EU market access | Post-Brexit barriers | Full EU access |
| SEPA payments | Separate from EU | Full SEPA access |
| Annual filing | Companies House + HMRC | Registrar + Tax Dept |

**Key advantage:** With a Cyprus Ltd, you pay 15% CIT and 0% on dividends (Non-Dom) = **effective total ~15%**. With a UK Ltd on higher profits: 25% CIT + up to 39.35% dividend tax = **effective total ~55%**.

### Who Benefits:

- IT companies with international clients
- Holding structures
- Trading companies (IP, licenses)
- Post-Brexit British businesses wanting EU market access

### Important Considerations:

- Company must have **substance in Cyprus** (real presence)
- "Empty" companies without employees may raise questions
- Since 2024, increased attention to **tax residency** of companies

## What are the alternatives to a Cyprus company?

If Cyprus registration isn't suitable:

### Estonian e-Residency Company:

- 0% tax while profits are reinvested
- Fully online management
- Account in a European bank

### UAE Company:

- 0% corporate tax (for most, but 9% above AED 375,000 since June 2023)
- Easier banking for some nationalities
- But no access to SEPA

### Keeping a Sole Proprietorship or UK Ltd:

- Working with foreign clients through your existing entity
- Receiving income outside Cyprus
- But there are nuances with tax residency — if you're a Cyprus tax resident, worldwide income must be declared. See [tax return filing](/en/business/tax-return-filing-cyprus)

## What practical tips should I know?

1. **Before registration** consult with an immigration lawyer about the impact on your status
2. **Don't register a company** in your name if you're on a BCS visa
3. **Calculate the full cost** — registration is cheap, maintenance is expensive
4. **Check substance** — empty companies attract attention
5. **Work with trusted accountants** — penalties for errors are high
