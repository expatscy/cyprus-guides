> **Short answer:** In 2026, the main working routes for Russia-to-Cyprus transfers are Profee (1.5-3%), KoronaPay, and crypto (USDT via P2P). Direct SWIFT between Russian and Cyprus banks is blocked by sanctions.


After sanctions were imposed, direct transfers from Russian banks to Cyprus don't work. If you also need general information about [transferring money to Cyprus](/en/business/money-transfer-cyprus-swift-crypto/), see our broader guide. Let's look at working alternatives from Russia specifically.

## What is the problem?

### Why Direct Transfers Don't Work

- Russian banks are **under sanctions**
- SWIFT transfers are **blocked**
- Cyprus banks **don't accept** transfers from Russia

### What This Means

- You can't simply transfer money from Sberbank to Bank of Cyprus
- **Workarounds** are needed
- Each path has its own **risks and fees**

## What transfer methods are available?

### Through Third Countries

**Popular options:**

**Kazakhstan:**
- Open an account in a Kazakh bank
- Transfer from Russia to Kazakhstan
- Then from Kazakhstan to Cyprus

**Armenia:**
- Similar to Kazakhstan
- Some banks work with Russians

**Georgia:**
- Banks accept Russians
- Can transfer onward

**UAE:**
- Harder to open an account
- But works for large amounts

### For Real Estate Purchase

When [buying property](/en/property/buying-property-cyprus/), money must come **from abroad**:

> "Has anyone had experience transferring money for property purchase in Cyprus from Russia?"

**Working options:**

- Transfer from a **Kazakh account** to Cyprus — accepted
- **Payment documents** needed to confirm origin

### Cryptocurrency

**Scheme:**

1. Buy crypto in Russia (P2P or exchanges)
2. Transfer to an international exchange
3. Sell and withdraw to a Cyprus/European account

**Risks:**

- Exchange rate volatility
- Fees at every step
- Questions from banks about source of funds

**For large amounts:**

- Need to be ready to explain **source of funds**
- Keep **all confirmations** of transactions

### Cash

**Import rules:**

- Up to **€10,000** — no declaration required
- Above — must **declare** at customs

**Risks:**

- Physical risk of carrying
- Questions about origin
- Difficulties with large amounts

## Can I use Revolut and Wise in Cyprus?

### As an Alternative

Many Russians use:

- **Revolut** — European IBAN
- **Wise** — multi-currency account

### Restrictions for Russians

- Revolut **doesn't open** new accounts for Russian citizens (since 2022)
- Existing accounts — work with restrictions
- Wise — similar situation

### If You Have a Residence Permit

With a Cyprus [residence permit](/en/immigration/residence-permit-cyprus-guide/), you can:

- Open Revolut as a **Cyprus resident** (not Russian)
- Use a Cyprus address

## What do I need for everyday life?

### Union Pay Cards

Some Russian banks issue **Union Pay**:

- Accepted in some stores
- Work in ATMs
- But not everywhere

### MIR Cards

**Not accepted** in Cyprus.

### Solution

Most expats:

- Transfer through third countries **every few months**
- Keep funds in Cyprus/European account
- Use Cyprus cards for daily expenses

## What proof of source of funds is needed?

### Why It's Needed

Cyprus banks and authorities may ask:

- Where did the money come from?
- How was it earned?
- Why did it arrive this way?

### What to Prepare

- **Tax certificates** or equivalents
- Property **sale contracts**
- Russian bank **statements**
- **Tax returns**

### For Real Estate

When buying property, **especially important**:

- Show legal source
- Confirm the money trail
- Keep all payment documents

## What fees and losses should I expect?

### At Each Step

- Converting rubles → third country currency
- Transfer to third country
- Converting to euros
- Transfer to Cyprus

**Total:** can lose **5-15%** of the amount on fees and exchange rates.

### How to Minimize

- Compare rates
- Transfer large amounts (fewer transactions)
- Use P2P where possible

## What practical tips should I know?

1. **Open an account in a third country** — Kazakhstan or Armenia is easier
2. **Save all documents** — every transaction
3. **Prepare for questions** — explanations for the bank
4. **Don't rush** — better slower but more reliable
5. **Consult specialists** — the situation changes
6. **Verify current rates** — tax rates and thresholds are updated annually. Cross-check with the [Tax Department](https://taxportal.mof.gov.cy/) before filing any applications
7. **Save all correspondence** — emails and SMS from Tax Department, Social Insurance, and Migration are evidence in case of disputes
8. **Plan your visit** — government offices operate 07:30-14:30. Book via [Ariadne Portal](https://eforms.eservices.cyprus.gov.cy/) or arrive at opening time
9. **Ask the community** — get real-time tips on specific situations in our [Telegram channel](https://t.me/expatscy)

## Key Takeaways

- **GeSY contributions are automatic** — if you are registered in the healthcare system, contributions are deducted from your salary. Verify your employer is withholding the correct amounts
- **Bookkeeping is mandatory** — even for sole traders and self-employed. Penalty for non-compliance: up to EUR 17,000. Hire an [ICPAC](https://www.icpac.org.cy/)-licensed accountant
- **Double taxation treaties** — Cyprus has agreements with 60+ countries. Check whether the relevant treaty applies to your income sources
- **Document retention** — tax records must be kept for a minimum of 6 years, including electronic records
- **Go digital** — most tax procedures are available through [TAXISnet](https://taxisnet.mof.gov.cy/). Register in advance — activation takes a few days
