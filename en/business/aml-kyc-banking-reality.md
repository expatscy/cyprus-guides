> **In short:** Since 2022, Cypriot banks have tightened checks for Russians. Bank of Cyprus has closed more than 20,000 accounts. The process involves AML/KYC checks and a document package, and can take several weeks.

Opening a [bank account in Cyprus](/en/business/banks-cyprus-comparison/) as a Russian citizen is doable, but not trivial. Since 2022 Cypriot banks have tightened checks on clients from Russia: Bank of Cyprus has closed more than 20,000 Russian accounts, and other banks have followed. Here is how the AML/KYC system works in practice and what you need to get through it.

> **Official sources:**
> - [Central Bank of Cyprus — AML/CFT](https://www.centralbank.cy/en/licensing-supervision/anti-money-laundering) — Central Bank of Cyprus AML requirements
> - [EU Anti-Money Laundering Directives](https://finance.ec.europa.eu/financial-crime/anti-money-laundering-and-countering-financing-terrorism_en) — EU AML directives (including 6AMLD)
> - [EU Sanctions Map](https://www.sanctionsmap.eu/) — current EU sanctions map

## What are AML/KYC and why do they matter?

**KYC** (Know Your Customer) is customer identification. The bank must know who you are.

**AML** (Anti-Money Laundering) is the fight against money laundering. The bank must verify that your money is legal.

In Cyprus these procedures are governed by:

- **6AMLD** (Directive (EU) 2018/1673) — the EU's sixth anti-money-laundering directive, which Cyprus must implement
- **Cypriot Law N. 188(I)/2007** — the main AML law (Prevention and Suppression of Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Law), with numerous amendments
- **Central Bank of Cyprus directives** — the regulator issues specific instructions to banks

For a bank, the fine for missing a suspicious transaction runs to millions of euros. For a manager, it means criminal liability. That is why the system is tuned to "better to refuse than to take a risk".

## What do banks check when you open an account?

### Standard checks (all clients)

- **Passport** — validity, authenticity
- **Proof of address** — a utility bill or rental contract (not older than 3 months)
- **Source of income** — employment contract, salary certificate, tax return
- **Purpose of the account** — why you need a Cypriot bank

### Enhanced due diligence (EDD)

For Russian citizens, banks almost always apply EDD. That means extra documents and questions:

- **Source of Funds** — not just "salary", but specific statements, contracts and returns confirming where each large sum came from
- **Source of Wealth** — how you built up what you have: sale of property, inheritance, a business. With documents
- **Tax residency** — where you pay taxes, whether you hold a Cypriot residence permit
- **Links to sanctioned persons or organisations** — checked against specialised databases

### What triggers "red flags"

The following factors raise the likelihood of enhanced checks or a refusal:

| Factor | Risk level |
|--------|------------|
| Russian citizenship (on its own) | Elevated |
| Large unexplained sums (>€50 000) | High |
| Transfers from/through several jurisdictions | High |
| Crypto income without documentation | High |
| Links to sanctioned banks (Sber, VTB, Alfa) | Critical |
| Activity tied to Russia's defence, oil or public sector | Critical |
| PEP status (see below) | High |
| No Cypriot residence permit | Elevated |
| Cash without proof of origin | High |

## How does PEP screening work in practice?

**PEP** (Politically Exposed Person) is a person with a prominent public function. That is not only officials, but also:

- Members of parliament and government, judges, senior military officers
- Heads of state-owned companies and state banks
- **Family members** of a PEP (spouses, children, parents)
- **Close business associates** of a PEP

> **Important:** If your former employer is a state company (Gazprom, Rostelecom, Russian Railways, etc.), that does NOT automatically make you a PEP. But the bank may ask additional questions about the nature of your work and your links with management.

PEP status means enhanced checks for a long time. Even after leaving office, PEP status lasts at least 12 months (under 6AMLD), and in practice many banks apply it indefinitely.

Banks use specialised databases (World-Check by Refinitiv/LSEG, Dow Jones Risk & Compliance, LexisNexis) for automatic screening. If your name matches someone in the database, a manual review follows.

## Which EU sanctions affect ordinary citizens?

### What is sanctioned

- **Specific banks** (SDN list): Sberbank, VTB, Alfa-Bank, Promsvyazbank, Otkritie, Sovcombank, Rosselkhozbank and others — transfers from these banks are not possible
- **Specific individuals** — about 2,200 people (current list at [sanctionsmap.eu](https://www.sanctionsmap.eu/))
- **The Mir/SBP system** — from 25 January 2026 (EU's 19th sanctions package)
- **Sectoral restrictions** — defence, energy, dual-use goods

### What is NOT sanctioned

- **Russian citizenship** as such — NOT grounds for refusal. That would be discrimination on grounds of nationality (a breach of the EU Charter of Fundamental Rights)
- **Personal funds** — your lawful income is not frozen (unless you are on a sanctions list)
- **Non-sanctioned banks** — Raiffeisenbank Russia, UniCredit Bank Russia and OTP Bank Russia formally still carry out SWIFT transfers (with restrictions)

> **Important:** There is a difference between the legal right and banking practice. Legally, a bank cannot refuse you solely because of citizenship. In practice, banks apply "derisking" and may refuse without giving reasons, citing internal policies. Challenging that is hard.

## What is derisking and why do banks refuse?

**Derisking** is a bank strategy of turning away whole categories of clients instead of assessing risk case by case. The Central Bank of Cyprus and the ECB criticise the practice, but banks keep using it because:

- The fine for an AML breach is up to **€5 million** or **10% of annual turnover** (under 6AMLD)
- Reputational risk
- Running a compliance check on each client from Russia costs more than the potential income

### Which banks are more or less open

Banks' specific policies change, but the general picture as of early 2026:

- **Bank of Cyprus** — the strictest compliance. Mass closures of Russians' accounts since 2022. Opening a new account is hard, but possible with a full document package and a Cypriot residence permit
- **Eurobank Limited** (formerly Hellenic Bank) — medium strictness. Depends heavily on the specific branch and manager
- **Alpha Bank Cyprus (ex-AstroBank)** — AstroBank's operations moved to Alpha Bank Cyprus in 2025; current policy depends on the client's profile and KYC documents

## Which documents should you prepare?

### Mandatory package

1. **Passport** (original + copy)
2. **Cypriot residence permit** (ARC — the plastic card). Without it, opening an account is extremely difficult
3. **Proof of address** — a utility bill (EAC, CYTA, Sewerage Board) or rental contract. Not older than 3 months
4. **Employment contract** in Cyprus or proof of self-employment
5. **Payslips** for the last 3 months

### For enhanced checks (EDD)

6. **Bank statements** for 6–12 months (from any bank — Cypriot, European, Russian)
7. **Tax returns** — Cypriot TD59 or Russian 3-NDFL
8. **Property documents** — if you cite a sale as the source of funds
9. **CV / LinkedIn** — banks sometimes ask for it to understand what you do
10. **Description of planned transactions** — approximate amounts and frequency of transfers

### For a business account (additionally)

- Certificate of company registration
- Memorandum & Articles of Association
- Register of directors and shareholders
- Business plan or description of activity
- UBO Declaration (ultimate beneficial owner)
- Documents on the source of investment

## What are the typical reasons for refusal?

1. **Insufficient proof of the origin of funds** — the most common reason. "Salary" without payslips doesn't count
2. **Links to sanctioned organisations** — even indirect ones (a former employer came under sanctions)
3. **No Cypriot residence permit** — most banks don't open accounts without an ARC
4. **Crypto income without documentation** — banks see it as a high AML risk
5. **Profile and transactions don't match** — you declare a €3 000 salary, and €50 000 arrives in the account
6. **PEP status** without further explanation
7. **Refusing to provide documents** — any delay or evasion is viewed negatively

## How long does the process take?

| Scenario | Time to open |
|----------|--------------|
| Standard (residence permit + employment contract + clean history) | 1–3 weeks |
| With enhanced checks (large sums, complex source) | 1–3 months |
| Business account (new company) | 2–4 months |
| Reopening after a closure | 1–4 months (if they open it at all) |

> **Important:** The timelines are approximate. A particular manager can speed the process up or drag it out. Christmas and summer periods are slower.

## What practical tips are there?

### Before visiting the bank

1. **Gather the whole document package in advance** — an incomplete package means an automatic "come back later"
2. **Translate your documents** into English or Greek (notarised, if the bank requires it)
3. **Prepare an explanation of the source of funds** — in plain language, with figures and documents
4. **Check yourself against sanctions lists** — [opensanctions.org](https://www.opensanctions.org/) (free)

### At the bank

5. **Be honest** — lying during KYC is a criminal offence
6. **Don't push** — the compliance officer is not your enemy, they are applying the law
7. **Write down the manager's name** — if problems arise, it helps to know who you spoke to
8. **If you are refused, ask for the refusal in writing** — you may need it for an appeal

### After opening

9. **Don't deposit large sums in cash** without warning the manager — it is a trigger for a block
10. **Answer the bank's requests quickly** — delay = suspicion
11. **Keep all documents** — the bank may ask for re-confirmation after 6–12 months (periodic review)

## What to do if you are refused

1. **Go to another bank** — policies differ
2. **Contact the Financial Ombudsman** — the [Financial Ombudsman of Cyprus](https://www.financialombudsman.gov.cy/) handles complaints against banks free of charge
3. **Write to the Central Bank of Cyprus** — the Central Bank of Cyprus accepts complaints about unjustified refusals
4. **Alternatives** — Revolut (with a Cypriot residence permit), Wise and e-wallets can cover part of your needs

> **Important:** If you need a Cypriot account for the migration service (proof of funds) and the bank refuses, talk to a lawyer. The Migration Department does not accept Revolut, and a bank refusal may affect your residence permit.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can a bank refuse to open an account solely because of Russian citizenship?**
Legally, no: that would be discrimination on grounds of nationality. In practice, banks apply derisking and refuse without giving reasons, citing internal policies. You can challenge a refusal through the [Financial Ombudsman](https://www.financialombudsman.gov.cy/).

**How long does it take a Russian citizen to open an account?**
From 1–3 weeks (standard case with a residence permit and an employment contract) to 1–3 months with enhanced checks. A business account takes 2–4 months.

**Do I need a residence permit to open a bank account?**
Most Cypriot banks require an ARC (residence permit card). Without one, opening an account is extremely difficult. Use Revolut or Wise in the meantime.

**What should I do if the bank asks for additional documents?**
Provide them as quickly as possible. Delay is read as evasion. Prepare bank statements for 6–12 months, tax returns and a description of the source of funds.
