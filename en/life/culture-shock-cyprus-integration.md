> **Short answer:** Treat settling in as a practical checklist, rather than a promise that you will feel at home by a particular month. Cyprus Samaritans publishes **80007773**, daily **16:00–00:00**, with an **18-and-over** listening-service policy. For an immediate emergency, call **112**.

## How can I make daily arrangements clearer?

Start with the tasks that matter to your household. These are planning suggestions, rather than rules about how everyone in Cyprus behaves:

- **Appointments and errands:** Confirm the specific office's opening hours, appointment time and required documents before travelling. Ask for a written confirmation when a missed appointment would be costly.
- **Repairs and deliveries:** Agree a date, a contact method and what to do if the provider cannot attend. Leave some flexibility in your own schedule without assuming that a promise means a different date.
- **Official applications:** Check the deadline and procedure for your particular application with the responsible authority. Keep copies of what you submitted and any acknowledgement.
- **Housing and noise:** If possible, visit a prospective home at different times. Ask the landlord about nearby building work, building rules and how to report a problem. Check the applicable rules with the local authority rather than assuming a universal permitted construction start time.
- **Meals and invitations:** Ask about timing and explain allergies, dietary preferences or when you need to leave. Decide which invitations you want to accept; you do not need to follow a fixed social script.

## How can I prepare for language needs?

Ask the particular doctor, school, office or service provider which languages it can support and whether your documents need translation. Avoid making that decision from a city-wide promise that every service will work in English.

The Ministry of Education's [Adult Education Centres](https://www.moec.gov.cy/epimorfotika/xenes_glosses.html) offer Greek to non-Greek-speaking adults. Check current enrolment arrangements and course availability. Our [Greek-learning guide](/en/life/learning-greek-language-cyprus/) explains the published options and their conditions.

As a personal learning plan, start with the phrases you want to use: introductions, addresses, appointment requests and questions about a bill. Choose a pace that fits your schedule.

## How can I build a social routine?

Try a small number of activities you actually enjoy, such as a class, sport or community event. Ask organisers about the schedule, language, access conditions and any charge before committing. If you want to volunteer, contact the organisation about its current vacancies and requirements.

Consider giving every household member a separate plan for the week. Discuss time alone, shared activities, professional goals and how to ask for support. If you are considering paid work, check your own permission to work before accepting an arrangement.

These are suggestions to try and review, rather than a guarantee of friendship, productivity or a particular adjustment time. Keep the routines that help you and change the ones that do not.

## When should I seek medical help?

Do not use an integration timetable to decide whether distress deserves attention. The [NHS's low-mood guidance](https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/feelings-symptoms-behaviours/feelings-and-symptoms/low-mood-sadness-depression/), reviewed on 29 July 2026, says that low mood lasting **two weeks or more could be a sign of depression**. It also lists loss of enjoyment, hopelessness, difficulty concentrating, and suicidal or self-harm thoughts as possible symptoms.

The NHS advises seeing a GP if low mood lasts more than two weeks, you are struggling to cope, or things you try yourself are not helping. This is general medical guidance; ask a clinician in Cyprus for an assessment and the appropriate local support. For an immediate emergency, call **112**; do not wait for a two-week threshold or helpline opening time.

If you are a GeSY beneficiary, ask your personal doctor about the appropriate service and access conditions. [HIO's outpatient-specialist guidance](https://www.gesy.org.cy/en-us/hiospecialistsaccess) provides both referral access and direct access to contracted specialists; direct access generally involves a personal contribution. Confirm the relevant provider, referral, charges and appointment availability rather than relying on a fixed waiting-time estimate.

## What does Cyprus Samaritans offer?

[Cyprus Samaritans](https://cyprussamaritans.org/) publishes these details, checked on 3 October 2026:

- **Telephone:** 80007773.
- **Call-centre hours:** seven days a week, **16:00–00:00**.
- **Service:** free, confidential emotional listening support for people who are distressed, lonely or unable to cope.
- **Age policy:** it says it cannot speak with anyone who tells the service they are **under 18**.

Ask the service about the language you need. For an immediate emergency, call **112**.

## Frequently asked questions

**How should I plan my adjustment to Cyprus?**
Use your own needs as a checklist: appointments, language, housing, social contact and support. Review what is working instead of treating six months as a deadline. Seek medical help if you are struggling to cope.

**Where can I start learning Greek in Cyprus?**
The Ministry of Education's Adult Education Centres offer Greek to non-Greek-speaking adults. Check the current enrolment arrangements and course availability; ask each service you use which languages it supports.

**How should I prepare for appointments and social invitations?**
Confirm opening hours, appointment times, required documents and deadlines with the particular office or provider. For invitations, ask about timing and explain any dietary needs or limits.

**How can I contact Cyprus Samaritans?**
Cyprus Samaritans publishes 80007773, open daily 16:00–00:00, for free, confidential emotional listening support. Its published policy excludes anyone who says they are under 18. For an immediate emergency, call 112.
