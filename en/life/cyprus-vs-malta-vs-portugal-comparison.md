Cyprus, Malta and Portugal are common Mediterranean destinations for relocating to Europe. All three offer warm climates, favourable tax regimes and a path to EU residency. But the differences between them matter.

## How do taxes work?

### Corporate Tax

**Cyprus** charges a flat 15% from 2026 (previously 12.5%). Dividends from subsidiaries are exempt. Foreign permanent establishment profits are exempt.

**Malta** has a headline rate of 35%, but through its full imputation system shareholders receive a 6/7 refund on tax paid, bringing the effective rate to **5%**. Since 2025, an alternative FITWI regime offers a flat 15% with no refunds (minimum 5-year commitment).

**Portugal** applies a standard 21% rate. Small businesses (up to €50K profit) pay 17% on the first €25K. A state surcharge of up to 9% applies to profits above €1.5M.

### Personal Tax and Non-Dom Regimes

**Cyprus ([Non-dom](/en/business/non-dom-status-cyprus-60-day-rule/)):** Non-domiciled residents pay no Special Defence Contribution on dividends or bank interest. The 5% SDC dividend rate from 2026 applies to Cyprus tax residents who are domiciled. The 60-day rule allows tax residency with only 60 days in Cyprus (instead of 183) if you have a business or employment there. The first €22,000 of income is tax-free (2026).

**Malta (Remittance basis):** Income not remitted to Malta is not taxed. Minimum tax on remitted income is 15% (€15,000/year floor). Ideal for people with offshore income who live in Malta but keep funds abroad.

**Portugal (IFICI / NHR 2.0):** The original NHR closed on 31 March 2025. IFICI offers a 20% flat rate on Portuguese income, with foreign income exempt. However, it is available **only** to: scientists, researchers and tech professionals in strategic sectors (STEM, healthcare, green energy). Requires an EQF Level 6+ degree and employment at a certified organisation.

### Tax Bottom Line

- **IT freelancer with a company**: Cyprus (15% CIT + non-dom) or Malta (5% effective)
- **Passive income**: Malta (remittance) or Cyprus (non-dom SDC exemption)
- **STEM professional**: Portugal IFICI (20% flat on local income)
- **Retirees**: Cyprus (€22K tax-free threshold) — Portugal NHR no longer available

## What visas and residency options are available?

### Golden Visa / Investment Residency

**Cyprus:** Purchase property from €300K for permanent residence. Processing: 2–3 months. No minimum stay requirement (visit once every 2 years). Schengen access: no (Cyprus is not in Schengen).

**Malta (MPRP):** Property purchase from €375K (or rent from €14K/year) + €60K administration fee + €37K government contribution + €2K NGO donation. Assets of €500K required (€150K financial). Schengen: yes. Processing: 12–18 months.

**Portugal:** Real estate route **closed** since October 2023. Investment funds from €500K remain. Processing: 10–12 months (AIMA backlog). Schengen: yes. Path to citizenship: 7 years for EU/CPLP nationals and 10 years for most others under the 2026 nationality law.

## How does healthcare work in Cyprus?

**Cyprus ([GeSY](/en/life/healthcare-cyprus-gesy/)):** Universal system since 2019. Contributions: 2.65% (employee) + 2.90% (employer). Covers GPs, hospitals, pharmaceuticals. HAQ ranking: 24th. Private consultations: €50–80. Note: not all residence permit holders qualify for GeSY — depends on permit type.

**Malta:** NHS is free for EU residents. Ranked 18th globally. Small islands mean fast access and short waits. Non-EU residents must carry private insurance.

**Portugal (SNS):** Universal system. Consistently ranked in the global top 20. Long queues in public facilities — many expats use private healthcare (€30–50 per visit). Lisbon and Porto have excellent private clinics.

**Cyprus** is the hottest and sunniest — warm sea from April. **Portugal** has the mildest climate (Atlantic influence) but the ocean is cool. **Malta** is a compromise, though humidity in summer can be uncomfortable.

## How safe is Cyprus?

**Portugal** ranks 7th on the Global Peace Index 2026 — one of the safest countries in the world.

**Malta** is not listed in the 2026 GPI ranking table. It has low crime rates, though petty theft occurs in tourist areas.

**Cyprus** ranks 80th on the Global Peace Index 2026. It is generally safe (Numbeo crime index: 32 for Nicosia), with low violent crime. Main risks: property scams, aggressive driving.

## How do language and communication work?

**Cyprus:** English is effectively a second official language. All government services are available in English. Over 75% of the population speaks English. Large Russian-speaking community in Limassol.

**Malta:** Maltese and English are both official languages. All documentation is in English. Over 90% speak English.

**Portugal:** Portuguese. English is common in Lisbon, Porto and the Algarve. Minimal in rural areas. Portuguese language proficiency (A2) is required for citizenship.

## Who should choose which option?

### Choose Cyprus if you:
- Run an IT business or freelance company (15% CIT + non-dom)
- Want hot weather and warm sea
- Need English as a working language
- Value a fast, simple [residence permit process](/en/immigration/residence-permit-cyprus-guide/)
- Work with clients in CIS, Middle East or Asia

### Choose Malta if you:
- Earn abroad and do not remit to EU accounts (remittance basis = 0%)
- Need Schengen access (Cyprus is not in Schengen)
- Prefer everything within 30 minutes
- Work in iGaming, fintech or crypto (strong ecosystem)
- Can accept dense urban environments with limited green space

### Choose Portugal if you:
- Have a smaller budget (cheapest of the three)
- Are a STEM professional (IFICI gives 20% flat rate)
- Prefer mild climate without extreme heat
- Prioritise safety (7th on GPI 2026)
- Want a 7-year (EU/CPLP) or 10-year (most others) citizenship path
- Appreciate culture, architecture and gastronomy

## Conclusion

All three countries are excellent choices for relocating to Europe. The key differences come down to tax and lifestyle:

- **Cyprus** — balance: moderate taxes, warm climate, English-speaking, fast residence permit
- **Malta** — optimisation: 5% corporate tax, Schengen access, compact island life
- **Portugal** — affordability: lowest costs, mildest climate, 7th on GPI 2026, but NHR is gone

For many expats with business or IT income, **Cyprus** offers a strong package: 15% CIT, non-dom status, English-speaking environment and a straightforward residence process. For a deeper look at [cost of living](/en/life/cost-of-living-cyprus-complete-guide/), see our detailed breakdown.
