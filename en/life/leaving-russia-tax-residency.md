> **In short:** When you move to Cyprus, you need to notify the tax office that you are no longer a resident, file your last return, be removed from military registration and arrange powers of attorney to manage your property in Russia.

[Moving to Cyprus](/en/immigration/moving-to-cyprus-complete-guide/) is not only about getting a [residence permit](/en/immigration/residence-permit-cyprus-guide/) and finding a home. A long tail of obligations stays behind in Russia: taxes, military registration, property, pension. If you don't close these issues, they will catch up with you. Here is what to do before you leave and after, step by step.

## How does the 183-day rule for Russian tax residency work?

### How it works

Under Art. 207 of the Tax Code, you are a **Russian tax resident** if you spent **183 days or more** in Russia within **12 consecutive months**. The days do not have to be consecutive — they are added up.

- Calendar days are counted (the day of arrival and the day of departure both count)
- The 12 months are not a calendar year but a rolling window
- However, for filing a return and calculating tax, your status is determined **at the end of the calendar year**

### Example

You left Russia on 1 July 2025 and never came back:
- In 2025 you spent about 180 days in Russia → you are a **non-resident** of Russia for 2025 (fewer than 183 days)
- In 2026 you spent 0 days → you are a **non-resident** of Russia for 2026

> **Important:** If you spent exactly 183 days in Russia, you are a resident. If 182, you are a non-resident. One day matters.

## What changes when you lose Russian tax residency?

### NDFL rates

| Type of income | Resident | Non-resident |
|-----------|----------|------------|
| Salary from a Russian employer | 13% (up to 5 million ₽/year), 15% (above) | 30% |
| Dividends from Russian companies | 13% / 15% | 15% |
| Sale of real estate (owned less than 5 years) | 13% / 15% on the profit | 30% on the full sale amount |
| Sale of real estate (owned more than 5 years) | Not taxed | 30% on the full sale amount (since 2025 — see below) |
| Rental income | 13% / 15% | 30% |
| Interest on deposits | 13% / 15% (with a tax-free minimum) | 30% |

### Key changes since 2025 (Federal Law No. 389 of 31.07.2023)

From 1 January 2025, non-residents selling real estate in Russia gained the right to an **NDFL exemption** if the minimum ownership period is met (5 years, or 3 years in certain cases) — on the same terms as residents. This is a significant benefit that did not exist before.

> **Important:** The law is already in force, but enforcement practice is only taking shape. Check with a tax adviser.

### Deductions

Non-residents are **NOT entitled** to most standard tax deductions:
- No deduction when selling property (apart from the minimum ownership period)
- No deduction for education, medical treatment or an individual investment account (IIS)
- No property deduction when buying a home

## Which tax returns do you need to file after moving?

### If you have Russian income

Even if you are a non-resident, you must file form 3-NDFL if you had:

- Income from the sale of property
- Rental income (if you file yourself, without an agent)
- Income from Russian sources on which tax was not withheld

**Filing deadline:** by 30 April of the following year (for 2025 — by 30 April 2026).
**Tax payment:** by 15 July.

### If you work remotely for a Russian company

Your employer acts as a tax agent and withholds NDFL itself. But the rate depends on your status:

- If you are a **non-resident** → the employer must withhold **30%** (instead of 13%)
- You must notify your employer about the change in your tax status
- If the employer keeps withholding 13% for a non-resident, that is its risk, and the additional assessment will come to you

> **Important:** Since 2024, income from remote work for Russian companies from abroad is treated as income from Russian sources (Federal Law No. 389). This means Russia has the right to tax this income even if you are a non-resident. The rate is 13/15% (if the employment contract is with a Russian organisation or its separate division), but with additional conditions. The situation is complicated — be sure to consult a specialist.

## How do you deal with military registration when you move?

### The obligation when leaving

Under Art. 10 of the Federal Law "On Military Duty and Military Service", citizens leaving Russia **for more than 6 months** must **be removed from military registration** at the military enlistment office.

### In practice

- Deregistration is done through the military enlistment office at your place of residence or through Gosuslugi (the gosuslugi.ru portal, "Military registration" section)
- You can apply before you leave
- When you return to Russia, you must register again within 2 weeks

### Consequences of not deregistering

- Fine under Art. 21.5 of the Code of Administrative Offences: 500–3,000 ₽ (increased since 2023 to 10,000–30,000 ₽ for certain offences)
- In theory, grounds for being put on a wanted list as part of mobilisation measures
- In practice, enforcement depends on the situation and your age

> **Important:** Deregistration from military records is NOT grounds for restricting your exit from the country. But failing to deregister can cause problems when you return.

## What should you do about your Russian citizenship when you move?

### Russia and dual citizenship

Russia **allows** dual (multiple) citizenship (Art. 6 of the Federal Law "On Citizenship of the Russian Federation"). Obtaining Cypriot citizenship, or the citizenship of any other country, **does not require** you to give up your Russian citizenship.

However:

- You **must notify the MVD** about acquiring foreign citizenship or a residence permit within **60 days** (Art. 6 part 3 of the Citizenship Law, as amended by Federal Law No. 142)
- The notification is filed in person or through Gosuslugi with the territorial MVD office at your place of registration
- Fine for failing to notify: 500–1,000 ₽ (administrative) or up to 200,000 ₽ / compulsory labour of up to 400 hours (criminal, Art. 330.2 of the Criminal Code — for concealment)

> **In practice:** If you live in Cyprus and do not visit Russia, you can file the notification on your next visit. Criminal liability is applied very rarely, but formally the obligation exists.

## Which powers of attorney should you arrange before moving?

### Why you need a notarised power of attorney

Without a power of attorney, you cannot do the following remotely:

- Manage real estate (sale, rental, privatisation)
- Represent your interests at the tax office, the military enlistment office or a bank
- Obtain documents (certificates, extracts)

### What to arrange before you leave

1. **General power of attorney** for a trusted person — broad powers, including disposal of property
2. **Special power of attorney** for specific actions — safer for large transactions
3. **Power of attorney to manage bank accounts** — if you need access to Russian accounts

### If you are already abroad

- You can issue a power of attorney at the **Russian consulate** in Cyprus (Embassy of the Russian Federation in Nicosia: [cyprus.mid.ru](https://cyprus.mid.ru/))
- A consular power of attorney = a notarised one, accepted in Russia without further certification
- Cost: from €30–80 (depends on the type)
- Appointments: through the consulate's website, the queue can be 2–4 weeks

## How do you manage property in Russia from abroad?

### Renting out

If you rent out an apartment in Russia as a non-resident:

- NDFL is **30%** of the income (not 13%)
- Options: (1) you file 3-NDFL yourself, (2) through a **tax agent** — a trusted person or a management company withholds and pays the tax for you
- You can register as an **individual entrepreneur (IP) or self-employed** — but there are restrictions for non-residents (self-employment is available only with registration in Russia and for certain types of activity)

> **Tip:** Consider setting up a **tax agent**: a trusted person rents out the apartment on your behalf, withholds the 30% NDFL and pays it to the budget. You do not need to file a return yourself.

### Selling

If you sell real estate as a non-resident:

- **Owned less than 5 years (or 3 years in certain cases):** NDFL 30% of the full sale amount (not of the profit — deducting the purchase costs formally does not apply to non-residents, although an exemption based on ownership period appeared in 2025)
- **Owned more than 5 years:** since 2025, an NDFL exemption (on the same terms as residents)
- A private buyer is **NOT** a tax agent. You file 3-NDFL yourself and pay the tax

> **Important:** When selling through a trusted person, make sure the power of attorney explicitly states the right to sell the specific property, with the cadastral number and address.

## What happens to your pension when you move to Cyprus?

### Pension contributions when working abroad

- If you work for a **Cypriot employer**, Russian pension contributions are not paid. Your Russian pension record does not grow
- If you work **remotely for a Russian company**, the employer keeps paying contributions to the SFR (Social Fund of Russia). Your pension record grows
- If you are **self-employed/an IP** in Russia, you can pay contributions voluntarily to preserve your pension record

### Receiving a Russian pension in Cyprus

- The pension is **not stopped** when you leave Russia
- You must confirm every year that you are alive (through a consulate or a notary)
- You can receive the pension into a Russian or a foreign bank account
- There is **no separate agreement** between Russia and Cyprus on mutual recognition of pension service (as of March 2026)

## What should you do before leaving Russia?

### Documents

- [ ] Arrange a **notarised power of attorney** for a trusted person
- [ ] Get an **EGRN extract** for all your real estate
- [ ] Request a **certificate of tax debt** on nalog.gov.ru
- [ ] Get an **apostille** on documents you will need in Cyprus (diploma, criminal record certificate, marriage certificate)
- [ ] Get a **criminal record certificate** (MFC or Gosuslugi, up to 30 days to issue)

### Finances

- [ ] Open an account at a **non-sanctioned bank** (Raiffeisenbank, UniCredit, OTP) — this is your channel for SWIFT transfers
- [ ] Move your main funds to this account
- [ ] Disable **automatic payments** from sanctioned banks (Sber, VTB, Alfa) — they will not work abroad
- [ ] Arrange a **power of attorney to manage accounts** if your trusted person needs access
- [ ] Download statements from all banks for the last 2–3 years — they will be useful for KYC in Cyprus

### Taxes

- [ ] File **3-NDFL** for the current year (if you have income that must be declared)
- [ ] Work out from which date you will become a **non-resident** (183 days)
- [ ] Notify your employer about the planned change in tax status
- [ ] If you rent out your home, set up a **tax agent** or a plan to file yourself

### Military registration

- [ ] File an application for **removal from military registration** (military enlistment office or Gosuslugi)
- [ ] Get confirmation of removal

### Registration

- [ ] Decide what to do about your **propiska** (registration at your place of residence). Deregistering is not mandatory, but it simplifies tax matters
- [ ] Notify the **MVD** about getting a Cyprus residence permit (within 60 days — you can do it on your next visit)

### Property

- [ ] Appoint a **manager** for your real estate (if you rent it out)
- [ ] Pay your **utilities** and set up automatic payments
- [ ] Take out **insurance** on your property

## When should you do each step when leaving Russia?

| When | What to do |
|-------|-----------|
| 2–3 months before departure | Powers of attorney, apostilles, criminal record certificate, opening an account at a non-sanctioned bank |
| 1 month before | Removal from military registration, notifying your employer, bank statements |
| 1 week before | Download all documents from Gosuslugi, make copies |
| Right after departure | Start counting the 183 days |
| After 6 months | You are most likely a non-resident. Notify your employer of the new status |
| By 30 April of the following year | File 3-NDFL for the year of departure (if you had taxable income) |
| Within 60 days after getting the residence permit | Notify the Russian MVD (can be done on a visit) |

## What are the typical mistakes when leaving Russia?

1. **Not notifying your employer of the change of residency** → the employer withholds 13% instead of 30% → additional assessment from the tax service with penalties
2. **Selling an apartment as a non-resident without knowing about the 30%** → an unexpected tax. Check whether the minimum ownership period (5 years) has passed
3. **Not arranging a power of attorney before leaving** → you cannot manage your affairs remotely. A consular power of attorney is a fallback, but it takes longer
4. **Forgetting the regular confirmation for your pension** → payments will be suspended
5. **Not saving bank statements** → problems with KYC when opening an account in Cyprus

## Frequently asked questions

**When do I become a non-resident for Russian tax purposes?**
When you spend fewer than 183 days in Russia in a calendar year. All days are added up (they do not have to be consecutive). The day of arrival and the day of departure both count.

**What is the NDFL rate for non-residents?**
30% on most income (salary, rent, interest). Dividends are taxed at 15%. Since 2025 there is an NDFL exemption on the sale of real estate after a minimum ownership period (5 years).

**Do I have to be removed from military registration?**
By law, yes, if you leave for more than 6 months. The fine for not doing so is up to 30,000 rubles. You can apply through the military enlistment office or Gosuslugi.

**Can I issue a power of attorney while already abroad?**
Yes, through the Russian consulate in Cyprus (Nicosia). A consular power of attorney is equivalent to a notarised one. Cost: €30–80, appointments 2–4 weeks ahead.

**Do I need to notify the MVD about getting a Cyprus residence permit?**
Yes, within 60 days. You can file it on your next visit to Russia. The fine for not notifying is up to 200,000 ₽ (criminal liability applies to concealment).
