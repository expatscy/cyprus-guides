> **Short answer:** The best Paphos neighbourhoods for expats are Kato Paphos (tourist area, walkable), Chloraka (British expat hub, affordable), and Tala (hillside, quiet). Rent is EUR 650-1,200/month for a 1-2 bed flat.


Paphos is the British expat capital of Cyprus. **40% of the population are non-Cypriots** (2021 census), and the largest single group is British -- roughly **25,000 UK nationals** call the Paphos district home. If Limassol is where you go for a career, Paphos is where you go for a life.

For British expats, Paphos offers something no other Mediterranean destination matches: a well-established English-speaking community, direct flights to **14 UK cities** from Paphos Airport, rent that is generally below Limassol outside premium beach areas, and the NHS-equivalent GESY healthcare system accessible via your S1 form. Add the Akamas Peninsula, wine villages, and 340 days of sunshine, and you have a retirement or remote-work base that consistently beats the Costa del Sol on both cost and community.

## What neighbourhoods are available?

Paphos is smaller and more compact than Limassol. It divides into the lower town (Kato Paphos / the harbour and coast) and the upper town (Ktima / the real town). Around these, a ring of village-suburbs: Chloraka, Tala, Peyia, Emba, Geroskipou. Most British expats live in the villages rather than the centre.

The key advantages over Spain and Portugal: English is widely spoken (critical for healthcare), direct UK flights are more frequent from PFO than from Faro or Malaga, and there is no language barrier for everyday life. The disadvantage: Cyprus is further from the UK (4.5 hours vs 2.5-3 hours).

## What are the neighbourhoods in detail?

### Kato Paphos (Lower Town / Harbour)

**Who lives here:** Tourists, young expats, digital nomads.

The seafront promenade, the medieval castle, the Archaeological Park (UNESCO World Heritage). Restaurants, bars, and cafes along the harbour. You can live without a car here. Kings Avenue Mall is a short walk away. In summer it is busy and noisy -- not unlike a Mediterranean Brighton or Torquay, but with better weather.

**Pros:** Walkable, seafront, restaurants, nightlife, Kings Avenue Mall nearby.
**Cons:** Noisy in summer, tourist-trap pricing at harbour restaurants, limited large supermarkets.

> **For digital nomads:** Kato Paphos is the only part of Paphos where car-free living is realistic. Cafes, coworking options, and the seafront are all within walking distance. If you worked remotely from Brighton, Hove, or Bristol harbourside, the rhythm will feel familiar -- morning coffee by the water, laptop at a cafe, evening walk along the promenade.

### Coral Bay / Peyia

**Who lives here:** Families with children, British and German expats.

The best sandy beach in the Paphos district. Lots of villas with swimming pools. TLC Private School is based here. Julias Backstube (German bakery) is a local institution. 15-20 minutes from the centre.

**Pros:** Best beach in the area, family atmosphere, school on your doorstep, a villa with a pool is genuinely affordable (compared to the UK).
**Cons:** Far from the centre, car essential, villa rents are high (EUR 2,000-4,000/month, GBP 1,720-3,440).

> **UK comparison:** A three-bed villa with a private pool in Peyia runs EUR 2,500-3,500/month (GBP 2,150-3,010). In the UK, that would get you a modest semi in the Home Counties or a two-bed flat in Zone 3. For families used to the South-East, Coral Bay offers a lifestyle upgrade that is hard to match anywhere in Europe at this price point.

### Chloraka

**Who lives here:** British retirees, quiet expats.

Between Kato Paphos and Coral Bay. A quiet suburb, 5 minutes from the centre. The most popular neighbourhood among British pensioners. Affordable coastal living without the tourist noise of Kato Paphos.

**Pros:** Quiet, close to the centre, affordable, near the coast.
**Cons:** No restaurants or bars to speak of, not much to do in the evenings.

> **For UK retirees:** Chloraka is the default choice for British pensioners who want to be near the sea without paying Coral Bay prices. The full State Pension (GBP 960/month) comfortably covers a one-bed here (EUR 750-950, GBP 645-815) with GBP 145-315 left over. Many residents walk to Kato Paphos along the coast road for shopping and socialising. If you previously lived in a quiet seaside town in Devon, Dorset, or North Wales, the pace of life will feel familiar -- but with guaranteed sunshine.

### Tala

**Who lives here:** Affluent expats, British and German retirees.

A hilltop village overlooking the sea and Paphos below. Home to **St Stephen's Anglican Church** -- the hub of the British community in the area. Popular with British and German expats for decades. Cooler in summer (elevation) and quieter than the coast.

**Pros:** Stunning views, peaceful, strong community, cooler in summer, quality villas.
**Cons:** Car essential, 10-15 minutes from the beach, steep roads.

> **British community hub:** Tala is where much of the British social life happens. St Stephen's Church runs coffee mornings, charity events, and is a genuine community centre. Walking groups meet regularly. Book clubs, bridge clubs, quiz nights -- if you enjoyed village social life in the Cotswolds or the Lake District but want to do it in 25 degrees instead of 5, Tala is the answer. The trade-off: you need to drive everywhere.

### Geroskipou

**Who lives here:** Budget-conscious expats, families, locals.

Between Paphos and the airport. Paphos General Hospital is nearby -- a practical consideration for retirees. Lidl, Papantoniou, and other supermarkets within easy reach. The most practical suburb for those who value convenience over aesthetics.

**Pros:** Affordable, hospital nearby, supermarkets, close to the airport.
**Cons:** No beach within walking distance (rocky coastline), not much entertainment.

> **Practical choice for health-conscious retirees:** If proximity to the hospital matters -- and for many retirees it does -- Geroskipou puts Paphos General Hospital within a 5-minute drive. A one-bed at EUR 750-950 (GBP 645-815) is manageable on the State Pension alone.

### Emba

**Who lives here:** Budget-conscious expats, Cypriot families.

A quiet village north of Paphos. The cheapest rents in the district. Traditional Cypriot atmosphere. If you want maximum value for money and do not mind being away from the coast, Emba delivers.

**Pros:** Cheapest neighbourhood, quiet, authentic village atmosphere.
**Cons:** Far from the sea, limited infrastructure, car essential, fewer expats.

> **Budget tip:** A one-bed in Emba at EUR 600-800 (GBP 515-690) is the cheapest option in the Paphos area. For a couple on a single State Pension, this leaves meaningful breathing room for groceries, utilities, and dining out. If you lived in a small market town in the Midlands or East Anglia and found the cost of living too high, Emba offers a genuine step down in price with a step up in sunshine.

### Universal

**Who lives here:** Young professionals, couples, renters.

A district of newer apartment complexes between Kato Paphos and Ktima. The practical choice if you need a flat rather than a villa. Modern buildings, mid-range prices, halfway between the sea and the town.

**Pros:** Modern housing stock, mid-range prices, between the coast and upper town.
**Cons:** Characterless, no neighbourhood identity, limited greenery.

### Ktima (Upper Town)

**Who lives here:** Cypriot families, creative expats.

The real town: the market, local cafes, narrow streets. Gentrification is underway. Cheaper than the coast. Authentic Cyprus rather than expat Cyprus. If you prefer Margate's Old Town over Marbella, Ktima is worth a look.

**Pros:** Genuine local life (not touristy), cheap, character.
**Cons:** No sea, older buildings, less English spoken.

## What should I know about Paphos Airport?

**58 destinations**, 17 airlines. For British expats, this is arguably the biggest practical advantage of Paphos over any other city on the island.

### Direct Flights to 14 UK Cities

Gatwick, Manchester, Stansted, Birmingham, Bristol, Leeds Bradford, Edinburgh, Glasgow, Newcastle, Liverpool, East Midlands, Luton, Belfast, and Cardiff (seasonal). Airlines include easyJet, Ryanair, Jet2, TUI, and Wizz Air.

This means you can fly home for a funeral, a wedding, or Christmas without connecting through Athens or Istanbul. From Limassol, you drive 65 km to Larnaca Airport, which has fewer UK routes.

Also: Berlin, Munich, Frankfurt, Dusseldorf (seasonal). Tel Aviv (year-round). No flights to Beirut or Moscow.

Source: [FlightConnections](https://www.flightconnections.com/flights-from-paphos-pfo)

> **Compared with other retirement destinations:** Faro (Algarve) has 10 UK routes, Malaga (Costa del Sol) has 18, but Paphos at 14 punches well above its weight for a city of 35,000. The flight time is 4.5 hours vs 2.5-3 hours for Spain/Portugal -- but the frequency and spread of UK airports is excellent.

## What British infrastructure exists in Cyprus?

This section does not exist for Limassol or most other Mediterranean destinations. It exists for Paphos because the British community here is large enough and old enough to have built its own layer of social infrastructure.

### Churches

- **[Ayia Kyriaki Chrysopolitissa](https://www.paphosanglicanchurch.org/)** (Kato Paphos) -- Anglican services every Sunday. The main hub.
- **St Stephen's** (Tala) -- Anglican. Coffee mornings, community events, charity fundraisers.
- **St Luke's** (Prodromi) -- Anglican. Smaller community.

### Pubs and Social

- **Pubs with Sky Sports**, Sunday roasts, and quiz nights in Kato Paphos and Coral Bay. This is not an exaggeration -- you can watch the Premier League with a pint and a roast dinner, served by English-speaking staff, every Sunday.
- Walking groups, book clubs, coffee mornings, bridge clubs, charity events -- all run by and for British residents.
- **Moufflon Bookshop** (Kinyras Street, since 1967) -- English-language bookshop. A Paphos institution.

### Practical Services in English

- Estate agents, solicitors, accountants, insurance brokers, and financial advisers who work primarily with British clients and speak native English.
- GPs in private clinics who are accustomed to explaining things in English (important for older patients).
- Supermarkets stock British products: Marmite, PG Tips, Bisto, HP Sauce -- all available at Papantoniou and AlphaMega.

> **Why this matters:** If you have spent time in the Costa del Sol or the Algarve, you know that English-speaking services exist but often feel like a veneer over a Spanish or Portuguese-speaking system. In Paphos, the British community is large enough that English is genuinely the default in many contexts. You can go weeks without needing to speak Greek. Whether that is a good thing is debatable -- but for retirees who are not going to learn a new language at 70, it is a major practical advantage.

## How does healthcare work in Cyprus?

| Facility | Type | Key detail | Phone |
|----------|------|-----------|-------|
| [Paphos General Hospital](https://www.shso.org.cy/en/hospital/geniko-nosokomeio-pafou/) | Public (GESY) | 127 beds, 72 doctors, hyperbaric chamber | 26-803-100 |
| [Evangelismos](https://www.evangelismos.com.cy/) | Private (GESY) | 79 beds, ICU, IVF, cardiology | 26-931-000 |
| [Iasis](https://iasishospital.com/) | Private (GESY) | Leptos Group, 24/7 | 26-848-484 |
| [St George & Blue Cross](https://www.bluecross.com.cy/) | Private | 50 beds, first ISO 9002 hospital on Cyprus | 26-946-232 |

Most doctors at private hospitals speak English.

### GESY vs NHS: What You Need to Know

**GESY (General Healthcare System)** is the Cyprus equivalent of the NHS. It launched in 2019 and covers all legal residents. For British expats:

- **S1 form holders:** If you have an S1 (issued by HMRC for retirees with qualifying NI contributions), Cyprus registers you with GESY and the UK covers the cost. This is your NHS entitlement abroad. Apply via HMRC before you move.
- **Working in Cyprus:** You contribute 2.65% of your salary (employees) or 4% (self-employed). Your employer pays 2.9%.
- **How it works:** Free choice of GP, referral to specialists. Broadly similar to the NHS model. Shorter waiting times than the NHS for most procedures (no 18-week targets because the queues are simply shorter). Smaller network of specialists -- for complex cases, some patients travel to Nicosia or Limassol, or go private.

> **The honest comparison:** GESY is not the NHS. It is younger, smaller, and less comprehensive. For routine care (GP visits, prescriptions, blood tests, minor procedures), it works well. For complex or specialist treatment, the network is limited in Paphos specifically -- you may be referred to Nicosia. Private healthcare fills the gap: Evangelismos and Iasis are both excellent, and private health insurance (EUR 1,500-3,000/year for a retiree) is vastly cheaper than equivalent UK cover. Many British retirees use GESY for routine care and go private for anything else.

## What communities exist?

### British (~25,000 in Paphos District)

The largest foreign community in Paphos and the largest British expat community on Cyprus. This is not a small outpost -- 25,000 people is the size of Whitstable or Lyme Regis. The community has its own infrastructure:

- **Anglican churches:** [Ayia Kyriaki](https://www.paphosanglicanchurch.org/) (Kato Paphos), St Stephen's (Tala), St Luke's (Prodromi)
- **Pubs with Sky Sports**, Sunday roasts, quiz nights -- in Kato Paphos and Coral Bay
- **[Moufflon Bookshop](https://moufflon.com.cy/)** (Kinyras Street, since 1967)
- Walking groups, book clubs, coffee mornings, bridge clubs
- British-run charities: Paphos Kidney Association, Friends Hospice, animal charities
- Multiple Facebook groups with active membership

### German (500-1,500 in Paphos)

A growing community, especially in Peyia and Tala.
- **Julias Backstube** -- German bakery in Peyia (orders Tue/Thu/Sat)
- German-language Evangelical services at Ayia Kyriaki (2nd Saturday/month, Sept-June, 16:00)

### Russian-speaking (~1,900 registered)

A small community compared to Limassol. Russian school Erudite + Morfosis (branch). Telegram groups exist but the social scene is thin.

## How much can I save compared to the UK?

One of the main reasons British retirees choose Paphos is cost. Here is how the savings break down for a retired couple:

| Category | UK (average) | Paphos | Annual saving |
|----------|-------------|--------|--------------|
| Council Tax (Band D avg) | GBP 2,171/year | EUR 0 (no equivalent) | **GBP 2,171** |
| TV licence | GBP 169.50/year | EUR 0 (not applicable) | **GBP 170** |
| Rent (2-bed) | GBP 1,000-1,500/month | GBP 690-1,160/month | **GBP 3,720-4,080** |

> **Tax note:** Foreign pensions in Cyprus can be taxed at a flat 5% on income above EUR 5,000/year, or at standard progressive rates (0-35%). You choose annually. For most UK pensioners, the flat 5% rate is substantially cheaper than UK taxation. The UK-Cyprus Double Taxation Agreement ensures you are not taxed twice. Consult a cross-border financial adviser before moving -- SIPPs, drawdown, and lump sums may need restructuring. ## What nature and outdoor activities are available?

Paphos has the best natural environment of any city on Cyprus. This is the main lifestyle advantage over Limassol:

- **[Avakas Gorge](https://www.visitcyprus.com/discover-cyprus/nature/nature-trails-2/avakas-gorge-linear-pafos-paphos-district-akamas-forest-nature-trail/):** 7 km, roughly 3 hours, 30-metre canyon walls. 30 minutes from Paphos. One of the best walks on the island
- **Akamas Peninsula:** Sea caves, the Blue Lagoon, Lara turtle beach. Protected wilderness
- **Diving:** 50+ dive sites. The EDRO III wreck, Amphorae Caves. Centres: [Cydive](https://www.cydive.com/) (est. 1978, PADI 5-star)
- **Cycling:** Coast road (harbour to Coral Bay), Akamas loops, Diarizos Valley
- **Wine villages:** Vouni Panayia, Chrysorrogiatissa Monastery (12th century), Tsangarides Winery. 10+ wineries on one route, 20+ grape varieties

> **For keen walkers:** The Paphos area has more accessible walking routes than anywhere else on Cyprus. Avakas Gorge, the Akamas trails, the Adonis nature trail, and the Aphrodite circular trail are all within 30-45 minutes of the town. Walking groups are one of the main social activities in the British community -- a practical way to stay fit, meet people, and explore the area.

There is no Tesco, Sainsbury's, or Morrisons, but Papantoniou stocks a wide range of British products (Marmite, PG Tips, Bisto, Heinz beans). Fresh fruit, vegetables, and local produce (halloumi, olive oil, honey) are significantly cheaper than the UK. Overall, grocery bills in Paphos run roughly 15-20% lower than the UK average.
