Birth registration, citizenship and immigration arrangements need separate checks. Start with the Cyprus birth registration and ask the relevant authorities about your child's circumstances.

## How does birth registration work?

The [Ministry of Interior's birth-in-Cyprus procedure](https://www.gov.cy/moi/en/documents/birth-certificates-birth-in-cyprus/) requires every birth in Cyprus to be registered **within 15 days**. You can register at **any District Administration Office**, regardless of where the child was born.

The doctor or midwife attending the delivery completes and signs the birth notification. One copy remains at the hospital or clinic, one goes to the District Administration and one is given to the parents. For a birth outside a medical facility, the doctor or midwife submits the form; if absent, the parents or another authorised person do so.

### Documents

The Ministry lists:

- Medical birth certificate — the birth notification form.
- Parents' identity cards or passports.
- Parents' marriage certificate, if applicable.
- For a child born out of wedlock, a joint paternity-recognition declaration or court order.
- For foreign parents, a residence permit.
- Completed application form.

### Fees and late registration

Each birth certificate costs **€5**. Registration after 15 days carries a **€30 surcharge**; after three months, the surcharge is **€60**, with an additional sworn declaration and witnesses. The certificate is issued after birth registration and can be obtained from any District Administration Office.

Ask the office about certificate language and spelling before requesting copies. Keep the notification and application records.

## Does birth in Cyprus confer citizenship?

Birthplace alone does not establish Cypriot citizenship. Under [section 109 of the Civil Registry Law](https://www.cylaw.org/nomoi/enop/ind/2002_1_141/section-sc852d7c40-bca1-5785-16b3-2a04cd997a2c.html), a person born in Cyprus on or after 16 August 1960 is a citizen if either parent was a Cypriot citizen at birth; the section also addresses a qualifying deceased parent. However, this rule does not apply where either parent's entry or stay in Cyprus was illegal, unless the Council of Ministers decides otherwise. Ask the Civil Registry about cases affected by that exception.

For foreign citizenship, check each parent's country's rules rather than assume automatic inheritance or compulsory embassy registration:

- **UK:** for births abroad on or after 1 July 2006, automatic citizenship requires a British parent who could pass on citizenship. The guidance also identifies an exception where the British father and the mother's husband are different people. Use the [UK government's citizenship guide](https://www.gov.uk/apply-citizenship-british-parent/born-on-or-after-1-july-2006) to check your case and the appropriate passport or citizenship application.
- **US:** one American parent does not guarantee citizenship in every case. Acquisition depends on the applicable parentage and residence or physical-presence conditions. A [Consular Report of Birth Abroad](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/living-abroad/birth.html) documents citizenship acquired at birth; it does not grant it. Check the [State Department's acquisition rules](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/legal/travel-legal-considerations/us-citizenship/Acquisition-US-Citizenship-Child-Born-Abroad.html) and the embassy's application instructions.

For other nationalities, ask the relevant consular authority what citizenship evidence and passport application are appropriate. Check each country's requirements if considering more than one nationality.

**M121 is not the domestic Cyprus birth-registration form:** the Ministry labels it [consular birth registration for persons born abroad](https://www.gov.cy/moi/en/documents/persons-born-abroad-consular-birth-certificate-form-m121/).

## Will I need an apostille or translation?

Ask the receiving authority which certificate, authentication and translation it accepts before paying for them. An apostille is not universally required. Under [Regulation EU 2016/1191](https://www.gov.cy/mjpo/en/exemption-of-certain-public-documents-from-the-apostille-requirement-according-to-regulation-eu-2016-1191/), covered birth documents issued by one EU Member State's public authorities and presented to another Member State's public authorities are exempt from legalisation and apostille. A multilingual standard form, where available, accompanies the original document; a certified translation made in an EU Member State must be accepted.

For another destination or procedure, confirm its specific rules. See the [apostille guide](/en/documents/apostille-documents-cyprus/) and request a translation quotation if needed. Check consistent name spelling across documents.

## What immigration arrangements should I make?

Ask the Migration Department which registration or residence application applies to your child's citizenship and your family's status. Confirm the deadline, required documents and whether an application may accompany your renewal. Do not treat the birth certificate as proof that immigration arrangements are complete.

## What maternity benefits are available?

The [official maternity-benefit FAQ](https://maternity-benefit.service.gov.cy/en/faq) sets insurance conditions:

- Actual basic insurance of at least **0.50 insurance units**, with at least **26 weeks** since insurance began.
- Actual or credited insurance of at least **0.39 units** in the relevant contribution year. For the first half of the year this is the second-to-last calendar year; for the second half, the previous calendar year. These halves begin on the first Monday of January and July.
- Payment is **72%** of relevant-year insured earnings for both basic and supplementary components. Only the basic component increases to **80% / 90% / 100%** for one / two / three dependants when the applicant is the sole household provider.

For childbirth, the benefit payment period is **22 consecutive weeks**, starting in any week from the **ninth to the second week before the expected delivery week**. Under [section 29 of the Social Insurance Law](https://www.cylaw.org/nomoi/enop/non-ind/2010_1_59/full.html), a third or subsequent childbirth adds four weeks; multiple births have separate extensions. These are benefit-payment rules. For payment, weeks run from Monday to Sunday. The law excludes payment for periods of full employer pay; with partial employer pay, the benefit is reduced so the combined amount does not exceed full earnings.

Not being employed does not by itself rule out entitlement: contributions and credited insurance periods matter. Ask Social Insurance Services to assess your record and circumstances.

## How should I arrange healthcare and vaccination?

For [eligible GeSY beneficiaries](/en/life/healthcare-cyprus-gesy/), covered inpatient services have **no co-payment**. Confirm the hospital, contracted service and your enrolment before delivery; outpatient pregnancy appointments have their own access and charging rules. Request an itemised private quote covering the delivery, room and newborn care, and written insurer confirmation for any planned treatment or existing pregnancy.

After birth, ask the HIO about your child's eligibility and registration, and arrange paediatric follow-up. Use the Ministry of Health's [2026 national vaccination programme for children and adolescents](https://www.gov.cy/media/sites/24/2026/02/NATIONAL-VACCINATION-PROGRAM-FOR-CHILDREN-AND-ADOLESCENTS-2026.pdf) with your paediatrician. The programme contains vaccine-specific schedules and risk-related conditions; a short age table cannot replace that assessment.
