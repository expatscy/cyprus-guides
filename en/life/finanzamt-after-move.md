> **In short:** After the move: deregister your German tax residence, apply for a Cypriot TIN, file your first TD59. Non-dom status saves tax on dividends and interest.

The move to Cyprus is decided, the flat is found, the flight is booked. But the bureaucratic side in Germany is often underestimated. Without a clean deregistration and clarification with the Finanzamt (tax office), you risk double taxation, back payments and nasty surprises with child benefit and Riester. Here is the complete checklist.

> **Official sources:**
> - [BMG § 17 — Abmeldung](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bmg/__17.html) — Federal Registration Act (Bundesmeldegesetz)
> - [EStG § 1 — Steuerpflicht](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/estg/__1.html) — Unlimited and limited tax liability
> - [DBA Deutschland–Zypern](https://www.bundesfinanzministerium.de/Content/DE/Standardartikel/Themen/Steuern/Internationales_Steuerrecht/Staatenbezogene_Informationen/Laender_A_Z/Zypern/2011-11-07-Zypern-Abkommen-DBA.html) — Double tax treaty
> - [Deutsche Rentenversicherung — Rente im Ausland](https://www.deutsche-rentenversicherung.de/DRV/DE/Rente/Ausland/ausland_node.html) — Continued payment and voluntary insurance

## What do I need to do and when?

### 3 months before the move

- **Consult a tax adviser** — clarify the tax consequences (in particular [exit taxation](/en/business/gmbh-exit-tax-planning/) if you hold shares in a GmbH)
- **Inform your health insurer** — mind the notice periods (GKV: to the end of the month, PKV: 3 months)
- **Inform the Kindergeldkasse** — notify the Familienkasse in writing about the planned move
- **Contact your Riester/Rürup provider** — clarify whether allowances have to be repaid
- **Clarify your pension insurance account** — apply for an account clarification (Kontenklärung) with the DRV

### 1 month before the move

- **Give notice on your flat** (if not already done)
- **Set up mail forwarding** with Deutsche Post
- **Check your bank accounts** — ask your bank about continuing them with a residence abroad and give your actual new address
- **Cancel or adjust insurance policies** — liability, household contents, car

### On moving day / within 2 weeks

- **Deregister at the residents' registration office** (BMG § 17)
- **Keep the deregistration certificate** (Abmeldebescheinigung) — this document is central to everything that follows

### 1 month after the move

- **Inform the Finanzamt in writing** — notice of departure and new address
- **Apply for tax registration in Cyprus** (Tax Identification Number / TIN)
- **GESY registration** (if eligible)

## How do I deregister at the residents' registration office?

### Obligation and deadline

Anyone who leaves Germany and keeps no residence there is **obliged** to deregister (BMG § 17(2)). Deregistration can be done at the earliest **1 week before** and must be done at the latest **2 weeks after** moving out.

### How it works

1. In person at the **Einwohnermeldeamt / Bürgerbüro** of your last municipality of registration
2. Show your **ID card or passport**
3. Fill in the form "Abmeldung ins Ausland" (deregistration abroad), stating your new address in Cyprus
4. You receive a **deregistration certificate** (Abmeldebescheinigung) — keep it permanently

> **Important:** Deregistration under registration law and tax residence have to be assessed separately. What matters for § 1(1) EStG is residence or habitual abode; a deregistration certificate alone does not determine your tax status.

## How does my tax liability change?

### Unlimited vs. limited tax liability

| | Unlimited (§ 1(1) EStG) | Limited (§ 1(4) EStG) |
|---|---|---|
| **Requirement** | Residence or habitual abode in Germany | No residence, no habitual abode |
| **Scope of taxation** | **Worldwide income** | Only **German-source income** (§ 49 EStG) |
| **Basic allowance** | Yes (€11,784 / 2025) | No (unless an application under § 1(3) EStG is made) |
| **Splitting tariff** | Yes (spouses) | No |
| **Special expenses / income-related expenses** | Fully deductible | Severely restricted |

### What changes in practice?

After you leave, Germany taxes only **German-source income** (§ 49 EStG), in particular:

- **Rental income** from German property
- **Dividends** from German companies (flat-rate withholding tax 25 % + solidarity surcharge, the DBA may reduce it)
- **Civil servant pensions** in principle in Germany; exception if you are resident in Cyprus and a Cypriot national (Art. 18(2) DBA)
- **Business income** from a German permanent establishment
- **Employment income** for work performed in Germany

### Informing the Finanzamt

1. **Write to the competent Finanzamt** stating:
   - Tax number and tax ID
   - Date of departure
   - New address in Cyprus
   - A note on the switch to limited tax liability
2. If you still have German-source income: the Finanzamt will move you to **limited tax liability** and may assign a new file number

## What applies to the final tax return?

In the year you leave, the length of unlimited tax liability depends on German residence or habitual abode (§ 1(1) EStG), not on the deregistration date alone. Once both connecting factors are given up, limited tax liability for German-source income may apply; statutory special cases and the DBA remain to be checked.

### Progression proviso

Even if your income is taxed in Cyprus after you leave, it can **raise your tax rate** in Germany — this is the progression proviso (Progressionsvorbehalt, § 32b EStG). Income that is taxed in Cyprus under the DBA is included in the calculation of the German tax rate but is not itself taxed.

**Example:** You earn €40,000 in Germany until June, then €40,000 in Cyprus from July. The €40,000 from Cyprus is added for calculating the tax rate — your German tax rate rises as if you had earned €80,000. Only the German €40,000 is taxed, though.

### Exemption certificate from Cyprus

To claim the DBA benefits you need a **certificate of residence (Tax Residency Certificate)** from the Cypriot Tax Department. You submit it to the German Finanzamt in order to:

- Prove the DBA exemption for Cypriot income
- Apply for withholding tax relief on German income (exemption application with the BZSt)

Apply in Cyprus at the [Tax Department](https://www.gov.cy/mof/en/) with proof of tax residence (rental contract, employment, utility bills).

## How do I cancel my health insurance?

### Statutory health insurance (GKV)

- Ask your health insurer to determine your insurance and benefit entitlements after the move; a change of residence alone does not end GKV cover in every case
- **Written deregistration** with the health insurer, with proof of the move (deregistration certificate, rental contract in Cyprus)
- Expectancy insurance (Anwartschaftsversicherung) is possible — it secures your return to the GKV if you go back to Germany

> **Note:** Check with your health insurer and the [DVKA](https://www.dvka.de/de/versicherte/rentner/) whether an Anwartschaft makes sense and which conditions apply on return; avoid cancelling before cross-border responsibility has been clarified.

### Private health insurance (PKV)

- Notice period: usually **3 months to the end of the insurance year**
- Inform the PKV about your residence abroad — some tariffs apply only in Germany
- An **Anwartschaft** is possible here as well — it preserves your health status and makes returning easier

## What happens to my pension insurance?

### Continuing to receive your German pension

The statutory pension is **paid worldwide** — including in Cyprus. You only have to:

- Tell the Deutsche Rentenversicherung your new address
- Submit a **certificate of life** once a year
- Provide your bank details (SEPA transfers to a Cypriot account are possible)

### Voluntary continued insurance

Even after you leave, you can pay **voluntary** contributions into the German pension insurance (§ 7 SGB VI):

- Minimum contribution: **€96,72/month** (2025)
- Maximum contribution: **€1,404.30/month** (2025)
- Responsible body: Deutsche Rentenversicherung Bund
- Benefit: higher pension entitlements, qualifying periods fulfilled

> **Note:** As an EU citizen in an EU state you can have contribution periods in different countries added together for the pension calculation (EU Regulation 883/2004).

## What happens to child benefit?

### Check your entitlement individually

Kindergeld can also be paid when you live abroad, for example if you are subject to unlimited German tax liability or treated as such, and in certain cases with limited tax liability and employment subject to social insurance. The Familienkasse checks the specific conditions; deregistration alone does not decide the entitlement.

### Exception: EU Regulation 883/2004

If, after moving to Cyprus, you **remain in employment subject to social insurance in Germany** (e.g. posted worker, cross-border commuter), your Kindergeld entitlement may continue. This is governed by the EU coordination of social security.

Report the move to the Familienkasse immediately and have your entitlement checked under the cross-border rules.

### Child benefit in Cyprus

Cyprus pays a **Child Benefit** — considerably lower than in Germany (no flat €250/child/month), but dependent on family income. Apply at the [Department of Social Inclusion](https://www.gov.cy/mlsi/en/).

## What happens to Riester and Rürup?

### Riester pension

If you move to an EU country: **no repayment of allowances** is required as long as you keep paying contributions and remain compulsorily or voluntarily insured in the German pension insurance.

**If you give up the contribution obligation** (no more German pension insurance contributions): the funding is **put on hold** — no new allowances, but no repayment of the existing balance either. Taxation is deferred until payout.

> **Important:** If you move to a non-EU country, allowances would have to be repaid. Cyprus is in the EU — the allowances are retained.

### Rürup / basic pension (Basis-Rente)

Rürup pensions are taxed on payout. Since the pension is paid from Germany:

- In the year you leave: contributions are taken into account proportionately as special expenses
- On payout: taxation under the DBA (Art. 17 — pensions in principle in the state of residence, Cyprus)

## How do I apply the DBA in practice?

The [DBA](/en/business/dba-germany-cyprus/) governs which country taxes which income. The most important cases after the move:

| Type of income | Right to tax | Note |
|-------------|-------------------|---------|
| Salary for work in Cyprus | Cyprus | Art. 14 DBA |
| Rental income Germany | Germany | Art. 6 DBA (state where the property is situated) |
| DRV pension | Germany may tax; Cyprus credits German tax paid in accordance with the treaty, to a limited extent | Art. 17(2) and Art. 22(3) DBA |
| Civil servant pension | In principle only Germany; only Cyprus if resident there and a Cypriot national | Art. 18(2) DBA |
| Dividends from Germany | Both (max. 15 % withholding tax in DE) | Art. 10 DBA |
| Interest from Germany | In principle only Cyprus where the beneficial owner is resident there; exceptions for permanent establishments and non-arm's-length excess amounts | Art. 11(1), (3) and (5) DBA |

## How do I get the certificate of residence?

To use the DBA benefits you need a **Tax Residency Certificate** from Cyprus. Requirements:

- Tax residence in Cyprus (183-day rule or [60-day rule](/en/business/non-dom-status-cyprus-60-day-rule/))
- Registration with the Tax Department
- Proof of residence (rental contract, utility bills)

Processing time: varies, usually **2–6 weeks**.

German bureaucracy when emigrating is laborious but manageable. The most important things: deregister properly, inform the Finanzamt, and document your tax residence in Cyprus as early as possible. If you handle these steps cleanly, you avoid double taxation and make use of the DBA benefits.

## Frequently asked questions (FAQ)

**Do I have to deregister in Germany when I move to Cyprus?**
Yes. If you keep no residence in Germany, you must deregister under BMG § 17(2) — at the earliest 1 week before, at the latest 2 weeks after moving out. Your tax status is assessed separately under § 1 EStG.

**Can I keep my German bank account?**
Clarify with your bank whether your existing account can be continued and report your actual new address. The EU right to a basic account applies to legal EU residents, not automatically to every other type of account; statutory checks remain possible.

**What happens to my statutory health insurance (GKV) membership?**
Clarify insurance and benefit entitlements with your health insurer before the move. The GKV does not end in every case simply because you change your place of residence; pensioners may fall under cross-border rules. Assess cancellation and a possible Anwartschaft individually.

**Do I have to file a German tax return in the year I leave?**
Whether a return is necessary depends on your income and filing obligations. Unlimited tax liability depends on German residence or habitual abode (§ 1(1) EStG), not on the deregistration date alone. Without either, limited tax liability for German-source income may apply; special cases and the DBA must be checked.

**Will my DRV pension also be paid in Cyprus?**
Yes, worldwide. Report your new address to the DRV and submit a certificate of life every year.
