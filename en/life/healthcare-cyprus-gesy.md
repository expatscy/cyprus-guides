> **Short answer:** GESY (GHS) eligibility depends on your beneficiary category. Paying contributions alone does not establish entitlement. Eligible beneficiaries enrol in the GHS Beneficiary Record and a personal-doctor list.

For a side-by-side overview, see [GeSY vs private healthcare](/en/life/gesy-vs-private-healthcare-cyprus/). Confirm your own entitlement and insurance terms before relying on either route.

## How does the GESY national system work?

GESY's outpatient stage began on 1 June 2019; inpatient services began on 1 June 2020. Its contracted services include personal doctors, outpatient specialists, laboratories, pharmacies and inpatient care. Covered services have referral and other access rules.

### Contributions

The [HIO financing guidance](https://www.gesy.org.cy/en-us/hiofinancing) lists these standard rates from 1 March 2020:

| Category | Rate and base |
|----------|---------------|
| Employees | 2.65% of salary |
| Employers | 2.90% of employee salaries |
| Self-employed | 4.00% of remuneration |
| Pensioners | 2.65% of pension |
| Income earners | 2.65% of applicable rent, interest and dividends |

The contribution base is capped at €180,000 annually per individual across applicable categories. For people who are not Cyprus tax residents, the HIO limits contributions to Cyprus-source income, earnings and pensions, excluding dividends and interest. Applicable exemptions require separate assessment; these contribution rules do not determine whether someone can use GESY.

### Who can use it?

The [HIO beneficiary categories](https://www.gesy.org.cy/en-us/hiobeneficiaryeligiblility) distinguish:

- **Cypriot citizens:** ordinary residence in Republic-controlled areas or the UK sovereign bases in Cyprus.
- **EU citizens:** ordinary residence in Republic-controlled areas and work there, or a permanent residence permit under the EU free-movement law.
- **Non-EU citizens:** ordinary residence in Republic-controlled areas and either a permanent residence permit or equal-treatment rights in social-insurance sectors under the Aliens and Immigration Law.
- **Recognised refugees and subsidiary-protection beneficiaries:** ordinary residence in Republic-controlled areas and the relevant protection status.
- **Dependants:** the HIO’s family-member conditions also apply. Family members of non-EU permanent-residence beneficiaries must themselves have permanent residence.

An ARC, a tax identification number or a residence permit alone does not establish eligibility. Ask the HIO to check your actual category, including any reciprocal-healthcare entitlement.

## How do I register for GESY?

The [HIO registration FAQ](https://www.gesy.org.cy/en-us/hiobeneficiariesregistrationfaq) requires enrolment in both the GHS Beneficiary Record and a personal-doctor list.

1. Check your beneficiary category and the required evidence.
2. Submit the enrolment request through the Beneficiary Portal, or ask a personal doctor for help. The HIO checks the Civil Registry or Migration Registry and, where applicable, Social Insurance records; it may request additional documents.
3. Select a contracted personal doctor online or by visiting the doctor. After an online request is accepted, visit the doctor to confirm your details and sign the mutual-acceptance form.

If your residence or employment circumstances change, ask the HIO how to update your evidence. The [HIO re-evaluation process](https://www.gesy.org.cy/en-us/hiobeneficiaryre-evaluation-process) can reassess or terminate eligibility when the legal conditions are no longer met; follow the instructions in any notice.

## What do beneficiaries pay for services?

| Service | Ordinary charge or condition |
|---------|------------------------------|
| Personal doctor | Free within the age-based annual allowance; €15 per excess visit |
| Specialist with referral | €6 for an ordinary visit; diagnostic-service rules and exemptions differ |
| Specialist without referral | €25 instead of the €6 co-payment; exceptions apply |
| Covered medicines | €1 per product; choosing a more expensive product can add the price difference |
| Covered laboratory tests | €1 per test or group, with a €10 maximum per category of lab tests |
| Accident and emergency visit | €10 co-payment under the ordinary GHS rules |
| Covered inpatient care | No co-payment for services supplied in the inpatient-care context |

The HIO’s annual co-payment cap is €150 generally, or €75 for Guaranteed Minimum Income recipients, low-income pensioners and children under 21. This is a co-payment cap; personal contributions, including the €25 direct-specialist charge and a medicine price difference, are distinct charges. Check applicable exemptions and the provider’s contracted service before booking.

Request a written private-treatment quotation for your service and check whether insurance requires prior approval. Ask the provider about availability rather than rely on a general waiting-time estimate.

## What should UK residents and visitors check?

Eligible Cyprus residents receiving a UK State Pension or another qualifying benefit may obtain a UK-issued S1. It must be registered with the Cyprus GHS. Eligibility is assessed individually; UK nationality or retirement alone is insufficient.

A qualifying UK GHIC or EHIC covers medically necessary state healthcare during a temporary stay, under the applicable local rules. It does not replace arranging healthcare cover when moving to Cyprus. See the [UK government’s Cyprus healthcare guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/healthcare-in-cyprus) and our [retirement guide](/en/life/retiring-in-cyprus-guide/).

## What should people with temporary protection check?

Under [section 20H of the Refugee Law](https://www.cylaw.org/nomoi/enop/non-ind/2000_1_6/full.html), people enjoying temporary protection who lack sufficient resources have access to medical care, including at least first aid and necessary treatment of illnesses. The law separately provides necessary medical or other assistance for people with special needs. When a person works or is self-employed, their ability to contribute to their needs is taken into account in determining assistance.

Confirm the current treatment route and required documents with the Ministry of Health and the hospital. Ask the HIO separately whether you qualify under one of its beneficiary categories; a change to another residence or employment status needs an individual eligibility check.

## What private insurance should I consider?

Request quotations for your circumstances and read the policy, including age limits, pre-existing conditions, maternity cover, waiting periods, exclusions, excesses and renewal conditions. Obtain written confirmation for any existing pregnancy or planned delivery.

For a residence-permit application, use the Migration Department’s checklist for your exact category. Ask the insurer to confirm in writing that the policy satisfies that checklist, including any required duration or repatriation cover. Check renewal requirements before cancelling or replacing a policy.

For a short-stay Schengen visa, the [Visa Code insurance rules](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A32009R0810) require at least €30,000 of cover throughout the Member States’ territory for the intended stay or transit, including medical repatriation, urgent medical attention, emergency hospital treatment and death. For multiple-entry applications, prove cover for the first intended visit and acknowledge that cover is needed for subsequent stays. Check the consulate’s accepted proof; a company name or a “worldwide” label alone is insufficient.

## What practical steps should I take?

1. Confirm your HIO beneficiary category and enrolment before relying on GESY.
2. Check the provider, referral, service and applicable charges before a planned appointment.
3. Keep your contact details and eligibility evidence current.
4. Request individual insurance and private-treatment quotations.
5. Check maternity and travel cover in the actual policy and relevant application checklist.
