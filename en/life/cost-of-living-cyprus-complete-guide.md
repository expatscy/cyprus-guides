> **Short answer:** Monthly living costs in Cyprus for a single person range from EUR 1,200 in Nicosia to EUR 2,500+ in central Limassol. Rent is the biggest variable, followed by transport and dining habits.


Cyprus is one of Europe's most popular relocation destinations. The cost of living here is significantly lower than in the UK — roughly 30–40% cheaper than London and 15–25% cheaper than most English cities outside the capital. Compared to other EU countries, it is lower than Germany, France, or the Netherlands, but higher than Greece or Bulgaria. Here is a detailed breakdown of all expenses by category and city. For the latest April 2026 prices and a ready monthly budget, see our [monthly budget calculator guide](/en/life/cost-of-living-cyprus-2026/). Moving from the UK? Our [Cyprus vs UK comparison](/en/life/cost-of-living-cyprus-vs-uk/) covers the specifics.

> **For UK expats:** All prices are in euros. At £1 = ~€1.17 (March 2026), your Sterling pension or savings stretches further here. Post-Brexit, UK citizens need a [residence permit](/en/immigration/residence-permit-cyprus-guide/) to live in Cyprus — but the process is straightforward.

## How does Cyprus compare to the EU overall?

Eurostat's 2025 price-level tables put Cyprus household final consumption expenditure at **89.2** where the EU average is 100. Food and non-alcoholic beverages were essentially at the EU average: **100.1** in the combined food-and-drinks index, and **100.2** for food alone. The pressure is category-specific: milk, cheese and eggs were high at 127.1, while vegetables were 90.6 and fruits and nuts 95.0.

Inflation in January 2026 stood at **0.5%** (CyStat data). Food prices rose 8.2%, while electricity dropped 6.5%.

## How much is rent?

Housing is the largest expense. Prices vary dramatically between cities and neighbourhoods.

### Average Rental Prices (February 2026)

| City | 1-bed (centre) | 1-bed (suburbs) | 2-bed | 3-bed |
|------|----------------|-----------------|-------|-------|
| **Limassol** | €1,100–1,650 | €900–1,200 | €1,500–2,500 | €2,000–3,000 |
| **Paphos** | €800–1,100 | €650–900 | €1,000–1,450 | €1,350–1,800 |
| **Larnaca** | €650–900 | €550–750 | €800–1,200 | €1,000–1,500 |
| **Nicosia** | €600–850 | €500–650 | €800–1,150 | €1,000–1,400 |

**Limassol** is the most expensive city — high expat demand, IT companies, and limited seafront supply push rents above Nicosia. A fixed 50% premium is too blunt: using the midpoint of Index.cy's 2026 asking-rent ranges for standard long-term 1- and 2-bedroom apartments, Limassol sits roughly 24–30% above Nicosia, while seafront, new-build, and fully furnished units can be much higher.

**Paphos** is popular with British retirees — lower rents than Limassol, English widely spoken, and a large expat community. See our [Paphos city guide](/en/life/best-neighborhoods-limassol/) for neighbourhood details.

**Larnaca** and **Nicosia** offer the most affordable housing options.

**Deposit**: typically 1–2 months' rent. Refundable on move-out.

## How much do groceries cost?

Eurostat's 2025 price-level data puts Cyprus food almost exactly at the EU average, but the supermarket basket is uneven. Dairy, bread, and oils are above average; meat, fruit, and vegetables are lower than the EU average. Locally grown seasonal produce is usually better value than imported or branded products.

### Monthly Food Costs

- **Single person** (cooking at home): €250–400
- **Family of 4** (cooking at home): €500–800
- Lunch at a simple restaurant: €12–15
- Dinner for two (mid-range restaurant): €50–70
- Cappuccino: €3.00–3.50
- Gyros/souvlaki (street food): €5–8

**Tip**: local farmers' markets (laiki) on Wednesdays and Saturdays can be good value for seasonal fruit and vegetables, especially near closing time, but there is no reliable blanket discount. For local produce, the Department of Agriculture's e-kofini observatory publishes producer and retail prices twice a week; compare by product and season.

## What are the utility costs?

Electricity is the most expensive utility. Cyprus relies on imported oil for power generation.

### Monthly Costs (85 m² apartment)

| Utility | Winter | Summer | Yearly average |
|---------|--------|--------|----------------|
| Electricity (EAC) | €80–120 | €150–250 | €100–170 |
| Water | €20–35 | €25–40 | €25–35 |
| Gas (10kg cylinder) | €16–22 | €16–22 | €16–22 |
| **Total utilities** | **€120–180** | **€190–310** | **€150–230** |

Summer electricity bills spike due to air conditioning. A poorly insulated home can easily exceed €250/month in peak heat.

### Internet and Mobile

| Service | Price/month |
|---------|-------------|
| Internet (fibre, 100+ Mbps) | €30–50 |
| Mobile plan (with data) | €15–25 |
| **Total telecom** | **€45–75** |

Providers: Cyta, PrimeTel, Epic, Cablenet. Fibre is available in most urban areas.

## How does healthcare work in Cyprus?

Cyprus has a universal healthcare system called **GeSY (GESY)**, operational since 2019.

### GeSY Contributions (mandatory)

| Category | Rate (on income) |
|----------|-----------------|
| Employee | 2.65% |
| Employer | 2.90% |
| Self-employed | 4.00% |
| Pensioner | 2.65% |
| Rental/dividend income | 2.65% |

Contribution cap: **€180,000/year**.

### Private Healthcare (optional)

- Private health insurance: €500–1,500/year per person
- Private GP visit: €40–80
- Dental cleaning: €50–80
- Dental filling: €60–120

GeSY covers most medical needs at no additional cost (GPs, specialists, lab tests, hospitalisation), but many expats add private insurance for faster access and broader specialist choice. For a detailed breakdown, see our [healthcare guide](/en/life/healthcare-cyprus-gesy/).

**For UK expats:** GeSY works similarly to the NHS but with lower wait times for specialists. UK retirees can also use the S1 form (formerly E121) to have costs covered by the UK — see our [retiring in Cyprus guide](/en/life/retiring-in-cyprus-guide/).

## What practical tips should I know?

1. **Farmers' markets (laiki)** — compare seasonal produce prices on Wednesdays/Saturdays; late-day stalls can be cheaper, but savings vary by product
2. **Air conditioning** — inverter-type units save up to 40% on electricity
3. **Solar water heater** — most homes have one; free hot water 8–9 months a year
4. **Bus vs car** — if you live and work in the same city, €50/month pass vs €200+ by car
5. **Lidl and Alphamega** — the most budget-friendly supermarkets for basics
6. **Renting through contacts** — often cheaper than agencies (no 1-month commission)
7. **GeSY covers the essentials** — do not rush into expensive private insurance; try the system first
8. **Summer electricity bills** — set aside an extra €100–150 buffer for July–August
9. **Get a car** — public transport is limited; see our [driving license guide](/en/life/driving-license-cyprus/) for exchange requirements
10. **Transfer utilities on move-in** — see our [utilities guide](/en/life/utilities-cyprus/) for step-by-step instructions
