> **Short answer:** Match the documents to the **origin, route and non-commercial travel conditions**. A titre test is **not required for every non-EU arrival**. When required, its sample must normally precede **certificate issue by at least 90 days**. Confirm Cyprus restrictions and arrival arrangements before booking.

## Which rules and journeys does this guide cover?

This guide covers dogs and cats travelling without a sale or transfer of ownership. Current EU non-commercial pet-travel rules are in [Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/131](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A32026R0131), applicable from **22 April 2026**.

The European Commission's [EU-travel guidance](https://food.ec.europa.eu/animals/live-animal-movements/dogs-cats-and-ferrets/travelling-pet-within-eu_en) explains these conditions:

- The animal travels with its owner and under the owner's responsibility. An authorised person can accompany it when the owner travels within **five days before or after** the animal; written authorisation must accompany the identification document.
- The ordinary limit is **five animals**. A single non-commercial movement normally uses one means of transport; the regulation makes an exception for public transport. The event exception requires competition, exhibition or sporting-event travel or training, written registration evidence, and animals **over six months old**. Outside the applicable conditions, other animal-movement rules apply.

Cyprus Veterinary Services' [published national guidance](https://www.moa.gov.cy/moa/VS/vs.nsf/vs07_en/vs07_en?OpenDocument) was last updated **10 January 2025** and still cites older EU legislation. Use the current EU sources for harmonised rules and ask Veterinary Services to confirm the national restrictions and arrangements for your journey.

## What identification and vaccination are required?

The current EU guidance requires microchip identification, with specified exceptions for a clearly readable tattoo applied **before 3 July 2011** and qualifying older identification. Ask the certifying veterinarian to check the identification against the current technical rules.

For rabies vaccination:

- The animal must be **at least 12 weeks old** when vaccinated.
- The vaccination date must **not precede identification or reading of the microchip**.
- Primary vaccination validity starts **no earlier than 21 days after completion of the vaccination protocol**. Subsequent vaccination must take place within the preceding vaccination's validity.

Cyprus Veterinary Services' published guidance says animals entering Cyprus must be **at least 105 days old**, and prohibits unvaccinated arrivals regardless of origin. Confirm age and vaccination documents before travel.

## When is a rabies antibody titre test required?

For non-EU entry, use the [Commission's current entry guidance](https://food.ec.europa.eu/animals/live-animal-movements/dogs-cats-and-ferrets/bringing-pet-eu-non-eu-country_en) and **Article 17 of Regulation 2026/131**. Listed-country exemptions are subject to the origin and journey conditions; a non-EU departure alone does not mean testing is required.

The exemption can cover a direct journey from a listed country where the owner habitually resides, a stay exclusively in listed countries, or transit through an unlisted country with the required declaration that the animal had no contact with rabies-susceptible animals and remained secured in transport or the international port/airport zone. Have the veterinarian assess your complete route and current country list.

When testing is required, [the current titre-test requirements](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_del/2026/135/oj/eng) specify:

- Blood sampling **at least 30 days after primary vaccination**, or within a current valid vaccination series.
- Sampling **at least 90 days before the date of issue of the animal health certificate**.
- A result **at least 0.5 IU/ml**, tested by a designated laboratory and certified in its official report.
- A satisfactory test does not need repeating if rabies revaccinations remain within the validity of the preceding vaccinations.

For a qualifying EU-resident pet's return, the 90-day period does not apply when the passport documents the satisfactory test carried out before leaving the EU. Ask the certifying veterinarian to confirm the return conditions; do not start the clock from the day you receive a result.

## Which travel documents apply?

**From another EU country:** normally a valid EU pet passport, completed by an authorised veterinarian, documenting identification and the applicable health requirements. Qualifying older passport models remain accepted under the transitional rules.

**From a non-EU country:** generally the current animal health certificate and a signed declaration of non-commercial movement. It must be issued by an official veterinarian, or by an authorised veterinarian and then endorsed by the competent authority. Specified passport exceptions apply to listed territories and qualifying returns from the EU; an EU passport is not automatically sufficient for every non-EU resident's journey.

Under **Articles 18–20 of Regulation 2026/131**, issue or endorsement must be **no more than ten days before EU entry**, with an extension corresponding to a sea journey. For onward EU movement, the certificate's validity is **six months from the entry documentary/identity checks or until rabies vaccination expires, whichever is earlier**.

[Implementing Regulation 2026/705](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2026/705/oj/eng) provides the current document models. The old 577/2013 certificate model is accepted under its transition only if issued **before 1 October 2026**, during the transitional period ending **31 March 2027**. For a newly issued certificate, ask the certifying authority to use the applicable current model.

## What Cyprus restrictions and arrival arrangements should I check?

Cyprus Veterinary Services' published guidance lists these prohibited dog breeds:

- Pit Bull Terrier or American Pit Bull.
- Japanese Tosa or Tosa Inu.
- Dogo Argentino or Argentinian Mastiff.
- Fila Brasileiro or Brazilian Mastiff.

It includes crosses between listed breeds, and crosses with another breed where a registered veterinarian certifies that the listed dangerous-breed characteristics predominate. The page also restricts cats outside the stated domestic-cat species, naming Savannah, Bengal and Caracat examples and hybrids. Obtain Veterinary Services' assessment of your individual animal rather than assuming a breed name alone resolves every case.

For third-country arrivals, the published Cyprus procedure requires notice to the **District Veterinary Office for the relevant entry point at least 48 hours before arrival**, with the date, time and flight number or ship name. Confirm the designated entry point and checks with that office.

The Cyprus page describes inspection of the animal and documents on arrival, admission when applicable requirements are met, and inspection fees for third-country entry or non-compliance. It describes return, quarantine or, when return or isolation is not feasible, euthanasia as possible responses to non-compliance. It also places inspection and quarantine charges on the owner or responsible person. Avoid treating quarantine as a substitute for arranging compliant documents in advance.

The published Animal Health and Welfare contact is **animal.health@vs.moa.gov.cy**. Use the district contacts on the official page for the actual arrival arrangements.

## How should I plan transport, costs and care after arrival?

Request written quotes and requirements for your exact journey. Ask about the crate, animal weight, permitted transport method, transit arrangements, document checks, laboratory work and arrival charges. Confirm acceptance before paying for your own ticket.

Arrange a veterinary consultation about your animal's individual care, vaccination record, registration obligations and eligibility for a pet passport. Ask about local emergency care and a suitable hot-weather plan. For later travel, check the destination's official entry rules rather than assuming the Cyprus documents cover every destination.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does every non-EU pet need a rabies titre test?**
No. Exemptions depend on the current EU country lists and journey conditions. Where testing is required, blood sampling must be at least thirty days after primary vaccination, or within a current valid vaccination series, and at least ninety days before certificate issue. The result must be at least 0.5 IU/ml from a designated laboratory.

**Is the health certificate timed from departure or entry?**
Current EU law requires issue or endorsement no more than ten days before entry into the EU, with an extension for the sea journey. It is not a universal ten-day-before-departure rule.

**Which dog breeds does Cyprus Veterinary Services list as prohibited?**
Its published guidance lists Pit Bull Terrier/American Pit Bull, Japanese Tosa/Tosa Inu, Dogo Argentino/Argentinian Mastiff and Fila Brasileiro/Brazilian Mastiff, including specified crosses. Confirm your individual dog's breed documentation with Veterinary Services before booking.

**How much should I budget for transport?**
Request written quotes for your animal and route from the veterinarian, laboratory if needed, carrier and arrival authority. Ask about documents, inspection, crate, transit and transport charges; this guide does not supply an unsupported fixed total.
