> **In short:** Cyprus offers Lebanese people an ideal alternative for escaping the banking crisis, with easy residence, investment and everyday life.

Cyprus is the European country closest to Lebanon, 45 minutes by plane from Beirut. The Lebanese banking system collapsed in 2019 and a string of crises followed. Since then, the island has been the first choice for Lebanese people who want financial stability and European security while staying close to home.

## Why is Cyprus the best destination for Lebanese people?

### Geographic and cultural closeness

- **45 minutes by plane** from Beirut to Larnaca, the shortest flight from Lebanon to Europe
- Several flights a day (Aegean, Middle East Airlines, Cyprus Airways)
- Shared history: the Cypriot Maronites are a constitutionally recognised minority who speak Cypriot Maronite Arabic
- Shared food: hummus, tabbouleh and grilled kebab are found in every Cypriot city
- An established Lebanese community, especially in Larnaca and Nicosia

### What Cyprus offers compared with Lebanon

| Criterion | Lebanon (2026) | Cyprus |
|---------|-------------|------|
| Currency | The Lebanese pound (98%+ collapse since 2019) | The euro: stable |
| Banking system | Frozen: withdrawal restrictions | Fully stable: EU-regulated |
| Electricity | 4-8 hours a day | 24/7 |
| Internet | Slow and expensive | Fibre, 100+ Mbps |
| Safety | Political and security instability | One of the safest countries in Europe |
| Market access | Isolated | EU member |

## How has the Lebanese crisis affected people in practice?

### The collapse of the banking system

Since October 2019, Lebanese banks have imposed informal (and later formal) restrictions on withdrawals and transfers:

- **Dollar deposits** are effectively frozen: withdrawals at a rate far below the market rate
- **Foreign transfers** are suspended or heavily restricted
- **Bank cheques** have lost their value as a means of payment
- The capital controls law (April 2024) set official limits

### What you can legally take out

⚠️ **The restrictions change constantly.** Check the latest Banque du Liban circulars before you do anything.

The general situation (2024-2026):
- **"Fresh Money" accounts** (new money deposited after 2019): transferable and withdrawable with relative freedom. Details: [Transferring money from Lebanon to Cyprus](/en/business/money-transfer-lebanon-cyprus/)
- **Old deposits** (before October 2019): heavily restricted. Withdrawals are in pounds only, at the platform exchange rate
- **Cash**: Lebanese rules on taking cash out can change, so check them with Lebanese customs and the Banque du Liban before you travel. Cash of €10,000 or more must be declared when you enter the EU ([Regulation (EU) 2018/1672](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2018/1672/oj), checked 2026-11-07)
- **Gold and jewellery**: no restrictions on taking out personal belongings, but large quantities call for a customs declaration

## How can you build a new financial life in Cyprus?

### Opening a Cypriot bank account

Since the Lebanese banking system is effectively frozen, opening an account at a Cypriot bank is the first step.

**Documents required:**
- A valid passport
- A residence permit (ARC or the Yellow/Pink Slip)
- Proof of address in Cyprus (rental contract or bill)
- Proof of source of income (employment contract, business profits or proof of assets)

**What Cypriot banks expect:**
- Banks ask about the **source of funds**, so prepare clear documents
- If your money comes from selling property in Lebanon, bring the sale contract and receipts
- "Fresh" money is easier to justify than old released deposits
- Be open about your financial situation: transparency beats evasive attempts

> For a detailed comparison of banks: [Opening a bank account in Cyprus](/en/business/banks-cyprus-comparison/)

### Property as a way to preserve capital

For Lebanese people who hold cash outside Lebanon or "Fresh Money":

- **Permanent residence**: buying a property worth €300,000+ gives permanent residence (Fast Track, decision within two months)
- **Smaller investment**: apartments in Larnaca start from €120,000-150,000
- Property in Cyprus in euros protects against the erosion of assets held in pounds
- Property transfer tax: 3-8% depending on the value (waived when buying a new property with VAT)

## Why is Larnaca the main Lebanese hub in Cyprus?

Larnaca is the first choice for most Lebanese people, and that is no accident:

### Why Larnaca

- **Closest geographically**: the international airport is in the city itself
- **Cheapest** of the coastal cities: a one-bedroom apartment rents for €500-1,000 depending on the neighbourhood (against €1,100+ in Limassol)
- **Arab community**: Lebanese restaurants, Arab shops, mosques
- **Finikoudes promenade**: similar to the Beirut Corniche (but calmer)
- **Major development projects**: the new Larnaca Marina and a planned new airport
- **A smaller budget**: a family of 4 can live on €3,000-4,500 a month

> More on the cost of living by city: [Cost of living in Cyprus](/en/life/cost-of-living-cyprus-complete-guide/)

## What is the role of the Maronite link between Lebanon and Cyprus?

The Cypriot Maronites are one of three religious minorities recognised in the Cypriot constitution. They speak a distinctive Arabic dialect and have a representative in parliament.

- **Historic Maronite villages**: Kormakitis, Asomatos, Karpasha, Agia Marina
- Most are in the north: reachable, but access is limited by the division
- **Our Lady of Graces Church** in Nicosia is the centre of the Maronite community
- This link makes it easier for Maronite Lebanese people to feel they belong

## How do Lebanese children move into education in Cyprus?

### From the Lebanese school to the Cypriot school

- **State schools**: free, taught in Greek, suitable if you plan to stay long-term
- **Private English-language schools**: €5,000-15,000 a year depending on the stage, easier for the transition
- **Arabic/French schools**: limited in Cyprus, and most Lebanese families choose English-language schools
- **Lebanese Baccalaureate**: not available in Cyprus, so if your children are in secondary school, plan early
- **Universities**: English-language programmes at a much lower cost than in Western Europe, €3,400-8,000 a year

## What are the routes to residence in Cyprus?

| Route | Requirements | Duration |
|--------|----------|-------|
| Work visa | A job offer from a Cypriot employer | 1-4 years (renewable) |
| Private company | Set up a company + work permit | Tied to the company |
| Permanent residence (Fast Track) | Buy a property worth €300,000+ | Permanent |
| Category F (financially independent) | Annual income of €9,568+ | Permanent |
| Digital Nomad Visa | Monthly income of €3,500+ from outside Cyprus | 1 year (renewable) |

## How can you handle Lebanese affairs from Cyprus?

### What you can do remotely

- **Power of attorney**: draw up a general or special power of attorney for someone in Lebanon through the Lebanese Embassy in Nicosia
- **Land registry**: buying and selling through an agent with a certified power of attorney
- **Civil status**: individual and family civil registry extracts, birth certificates, all through the embassy
- **Lebanese taxes**: Lebanon taxes only income earned inside Lebanon (a territorial system), so your income in Cyprus is not subject to Lebanese tax

### The Lebanese Embassy in Nicosia

- Address: 1 Agias Elenis Street, Nicosia
- Offers full consular services: passports, civil status documents, powers of attorney
- **An appointment is required** for most procedures

## What is the psychological side of moving from Lebanon to Cyprus?

You can't ignore the emotional side. You are 45 minutes from Beirut, so you can fly out in the morning and be back in the evening. But that closeness sometimes makes it harder:

- You see the news from Lebanon every day and feel helpless
- Your family and friends are still there, struggling
- Survivor's guilt is very common
- **The Lebanese community in Cyprus helps.** You are not alone: WhatsApp and Telegram groups, social gatherings and restaurants like those in Gemmayzeh and Badaro

**Practical advice:** allow yourself an adjustment period of at least 6 months. Don't compare Cyprus with Lebanon; compare it with the life you would have lived if you had stayed.

## Practical tips for Lebanese people

1. **Open a bank account right away.** Don't rely on cash for long
2. **Register with GeSY** (the healthcare system) as soon as you get your residence permit: full medical coverage
3. **Learn the basics of Greek.** It isn't essential for getting by, but it makes life much easier
4. **Don't expect** Cypriot banks to work like Lebanese banks before 2019: the system here is slower but stable
5. **Prepare source-of-funds documents** before any large financial transaction
6. **Explore Larnaca first.** Even if you plan to live in Limassol, Larnaca is an easier starting point

## Frequently asked questions (FAQ)

**Can Lebanese citizens open a bank account in Cyprus?**
Yes, with a valid residence permit. The main banks are Bank of Cyprus and Eurobank Limited. Prepare documents proving the source of your funds: banks ask a lot of questions about money coming from Lebanon.

**Do I need a visa to travel to Cyprus?**
Yes. Lebanese citizens need a Schengen visa or a Cypriot visa. Cyprus is not in the Schengen Area, but it accepts multiple-entry Schengen visas.

**How much does it cost to live in Larnaca as a family?**
€3,000-4,500 a month for a family of 4 (including rent, food, transport and private education).

**Can I transfer money from Lebanon to Cyprus?**
Only "Fresh Money" accounts (money deposited after 2019) can be transferred. Old deposits are effectively frozen. Details: [Transferring money from Lebanon](/en/business/money-transfer-lebanon-cyprus/).
