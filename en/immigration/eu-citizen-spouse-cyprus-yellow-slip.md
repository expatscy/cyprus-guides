> **Short answer:** Non-EU family members of EU/EEA/Swiss citizens exercising free movement in Cyprus use a **MEU2 residence card**. The EU citizen's **MEU1** is a registration certificate. Apply within **four months of arrival**; the official application fee is **EUR 20**. The card lasts five years, or the sponsor's shorter envisaged stay.

A Cypriot citizen living in Cyprus ordinarily uses the separate national family-member route, **CYE/CYV**. Returning citizens who exercised free movement abroad should have their individual route assessed.

## Who is eligible?

The [Migration Department's MEU2 guidance](https://www.gov.cy/mip-md/en/documents/e-u-e-e-a-citizens-and-family-members-2/e-u-e-e-a-citizens-family-member-2/entry-and-residence-of-third-country-nationals-who-are-family-members-of-an-e-u-e-e-a-citizen-meu2/) defines family members as:

- A spouse or civil-union partner
- Direct descendants under 21, or older dependent descendants, including those of the spouse/partner
- Dependent direct relatives in the ascending line, including those of the spouse/partner

For residence beyond three months, [Article 7 of Directive 2004/38](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=celex%3A32004L0038) requires the EU sponsor to qualify as a worker/self-employed person, or have sufficient resources and comprehensive sickness insurance, or satisfy the student conditions. A student sponsor must be enrolled at an accredited or financed establishment, have comprehensive sickness insurance and assure sufficient resources. Under Article 7(4), that student's automatic family residence right covers the spouse/recognized registered partner and **dependent children**; dependent ascending relatives fall under the facilitation provisions instead.

The Directive ordinarily applies when an EU citizen moves to or resides in a member state other than their nationality state. Marriage alone does not establish every residence condition.

## What is the Yellow Slip?

For the non-EU family member discussed here, the relevant document is a **MEU2 residence card**, distinct from the EU citizen's MEU1 registration certificate. The official spouse checklist requests a copy of the sponsor's MEU1.

The card and application certificate evidence rights; they do not create residence or work rights independently of the underlying conditions.

## How does the application process work?

### Required documents

Use the [official MEU2 forms and supporting-document lists](https://www.gov.cy/mip-md/en/documents/entry-and-residence-of-third-country-nationals-who-are-family-members-of-an-e-u-e-e-a-citizen-meu2/) for your category. Separate lists cover spouses, descendants, ascending relatives and retained rights after divorce/death.

The published **spouse checklist** includes:

1. A copy of the applicant's valid passport, with the original presented; a copy of the sponsor's valid passport/ID, with the original presented; and the sponsor's MEU1 copy.
2. The marriage or civil-partnership certificate. For a union concluded abroad, the checklist requests certification and translation and presentation of the original. For a union concluded in Cyprus it requests the relevant non-impediment certificates or a Registrar of Marriage certificate.
3. Children's birth certificates, where applicable.
4. A signed declaration of harmonious cohabitation at the officially declared address, certified by the relevant community/parish authority, and the EU citizen's affidavit assuming the spouse's expenses.
5. Applicable employment certificates showing duration and salary; social-insurance registration and contribution evidence for employed/self-employed sponsors; tax declarations where applicable. For a non-working sponsor, the checklist requests stable-income evidence and bank movements for the last quarter, with separate documentation for qualifying involuntary unemployment.
6. A title deed or rental document, original rent-payment receipts where relevant, and original electricity and water bills in one spouse's name. The rental document instructions include signature certification and specified landlord/tenant identification and contact details.
7. Plan A insurance covering medical care, inpatient/outpatient care and transportation of remains, **or** GHS registration evidence plus transportation-of-remains cover.

These are the department's published documentation instructions; ask which employment and income items apply to your circumstances. Follow the document-specific certification and translation rules. An apostille is not universally required: covered EU public documents and other applicable exemptions must be considered.

The checklist still mentions rental-agreement stamping. The [Tax Department](https://www.gov.cy/mof-tax/documents/chartosima/) says stamp duty was abolished from **1 January 2026**, while documents first signed by at least one party by 31 December 2025 remain subject to the earlier rules.

### Submission and biometrics

Use the **application-points and appointment links** on the official MEU2 page. The department instructs the EU citizen to attend with the family member and provide resources evidence.

Apply within **four months of arrival** and pay **EUR 20**. The department requests biometric photographs, fingerprints and a signature, with a valid passport and submission receipt. Children under six provide a photograph only.

Article 10 requires an application certificate to be issued **immediately**. It records the application and does not itself create work rights.

### Processing and collection

The Migration Department states that complete applications receive a decision within **six months**; its guidance says incomplete applications or missing documents extend examination and that interviews or additional evidence may be requested. Article 10 of the Directive requires the residence card no later than six months from application. Keep these source-specific statements distinct and seek advice if the legal deadline is exceeded.

Approval brings a collection notification; rejection brings a letter giving reasons at the last declared address. Keep that address current.

## What work and healthcare rights do I have?

Article 23 entitles qualifying family members with residence or permanent-residence rights to **employment or self-employment**. Article 25 says possession of the card, registration certificate or application certificate cannot be made a precondition for exercising a right: entitlement may be proved by other means. The documents evidence qualifying rights rather than granting them automatically.

GeSY eligibility is a separate assessment under the [HIO's ordinary-residence and beneficiary/dependant criteria](https://www.gesy.org.cy/en-us/hiobeneficiaryeligiblility). A MEU2 application or card alone does not establish every healthcare eligibility condition.

For another family-employment route, read the [spouse-employment guide](/en/immigration/spouse-employment-permit-cyprus/) and confirm its own conditions rather than assuming the same rights.

## What is the path to citizenship?

Marriage to another EU citizen does not itself shorten the Cyprus naturalization conditions. Qualifying residence must satisfy the legal-formalities and physical-presence requirements, and the applicant must meet the other citizenship conditions. See the [standard naturalization guide](/en/immigration/cyprus-citizenship-naturalization-standard/).

Spouses/civil partners of **Cypriot citizens** have the separate [M125 route](https://www.gov.cy/moi/en/documents/acquisition-of-cypriot-citizenship-due-to-marriage-civil-partnership-with-a-cypriot-citizen-form-m125/). The published resident-couple criteria require three years of marriage/union, at least six months in Cyprus each year and at least two years overall during the preceding three years. A separate category concerns couples permanently resident abroad. The June 2025 M125 checklist does not list the M127 Greek-language test. Eligibility to apply does not guarantee approval.

## What happens in case of divorce?

Under Article 13(2), non-EU family members may retain residence rights where:

- Before proceedings, the marriage/registered partnership lasted at least three years, including one in the host state
- The non-EU spouse/partner has custody of the EU citizen's children by agreement or court order
- Particularly difficult circumstances apply, such as domestic violence while the marriage/partnership subsisted
- A court has ruled that access to a minor child must take place in the host state, for as long as required

Before permanent residence, the person must also be a worker/self-employed, or have sufficient resources and comprehensive sickness insurance, or belong to a family already constituted in the host state whose qualifying member meets those conditions. Retained residence is on a personal basis. The Migration Department's guidance and checklist describe the national supporting-document requirements; have your circumstances assessed rather than assuming divorce automatically cancels or preserves the card.

## What are the frequently asked questions?

**Can I work in Cyprus with a Yellow Slip?**

Qualifying EU-family residents have employment and self-employment rights. The application certificate records submission; it does not create those rights. The underlying residence conditions must be met.

**How long does it take to get a Yellow Slip in Cyprus?**

Apply within four months of arrival. The Migration Department publishes a six-month decision period for complete MEU2 applications and says missing documents extend examination. Article 10 of Directive 2004/38 requires the residence card within six months of application and an immediate application certificate.

**Can I apply for Cyprus citizenship with a Yellow Slip?**

Spouses/civil partners of Cypriot citizens use the separate M125 route after three years of marriage/union. For resident couples, the preceding three years must include at least six months in Cyprus each year and two years in total. The M125 checklist does not list the M127 Greek-language test. Marriage to another EU citizen does not itself shorten naturalization requirements; see the citizenship guide for the applicable route.

**What if my Cypriot spouse works in another EU country?**

The ordinary national CYE/CYV route and EU free-movement routes have different scopes. Ask the responsible authority to assess your destination, residence history and return to Cyprus; do not assume an embassy application is faster or is the applicable MEU2 route.

**Does time on a MEU2 card count toward a passport?**

The card alone does not establish that every day qualifies. Residence must meet the citizenship law's legal and physical-presence requirements, as well as the other conditions for the applicable route.

**Can I travel within the EU with a MEU2 card?**

Carry a valid passport. The [EU's Your Europe guidance, checked 30 September 2026](https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/travel/entry-exit/non-eu-family/index_en.htm), says a valid EU-family residence card permits visa-free travel to EU countries, Iceland, Liechtenstein and Norway, including travel alone. It specifically excepts **Switzerland where the card was issued by Cyprus or Ireland**. An ordinary national residence permit is a different document; an application receipt is not the residence card. Confirm destination and return-document requirements before travelling.
