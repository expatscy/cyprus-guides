> **In short:** Cypriot citizenship brings military service for sons. Early planning and timing strategies can help you avoid a double obligation with the IDF.

For Israeli families who have moved to Cyprus, the question of Cypriot citizenship gets complicated as soon as there are sons. The reason: **a male Cypriot citizen is obliged to do military service**. Unlike in the IDF, you cannot simply "get out of it". And contrary to what many people think, IDF service **does not exempt** you from service in Cyprus. This guide helps you make an informed decision.

> - [National Guard Law 2011](https://www.gov.cy/mod/en/) — Law 19(I)/2011, service obligation
> - [Ministry of Defence](https://www.gov.cy/mod/en/) — Ministry of Defence
> - [Migration Department](https://www.gov.cy/mip-md/en/) — citizenship and naturalization

## What is the key decision Israeli families have to make?

### The dilemma

Cyprus offers a path to naturalization after **5-7 years of residence** (or faster through BCS). The advantages of Cypriot citizenship are clear:

- **EU passport** — freedom of movement, work and study in 27 countries
- **The right to vote** and civic participation
- **Security** — no dependence on visa renewals
- **Access to pay** on the same terms as EU citizens (no work permit restrictions)

But if you have **sons**, citizenship comes with an obligation to do military service.

### The basic rule

| Situation | Service obligation |
|-----|-----------|
| Son receives citizenship **before age 18** | Must serve at 18 |
| Son receives citizenship **at age 18-26** | Must serve (6 months) |
| Son receives citizenship **after age 26** | Shortened service (3 months) |
| **Daughters** | **Not** required to serve — Cyprus does not conscript women |

## How does the length of military service differ between Cyprus and the IDF?

### The Cypriot National Guard (Εθνική Φρουρά)

| Category | Length of service |
|---------|----------|
| Citizen by birth | **14 months** |
| Naturalized, age 18-26 | **6 months** (8 if deferred) |
| Naturalized, over 26 | **3 months** (5 if deferred) |

### The IDF, for comparison

| Category | Length of service |
|---------|----------|
| Men | **32 months** (2 years and 8 months) |
| Women | **24 months** |
| Reserves | **up to age 40+** |

### The key differences

- **No reserve duty in Cyprus** — once regular service is over, you are done (there is a theoretical standby obligation, but nobody is called up)
- **No combat deployment** — Cyprus does not send soldiers on operations
- **Basic training** — routine service, nothing special. No "elite units"
- **Service is shorter** — even 14 months for a citizen by birth is less than half the IDF's length

## How does the double obligation to serve in both the IDF and Cyprus work?

### The truth many people don't know

IDF service **does not exempt** you from service in the Cypriot army, and vice versa. Cyprus recognizes dual citizenship, but on its own territory you are Cypriot first.

### The scenario

A son with Israeli-Cypriot citizenship may be obliged to serve in **two armies**:

1. **The IDF** — service obligation for every Israeli (32 months)
2. **The National Guard** — service obligation for every Cypriot (3-14 months)

### Practical solutions

- **Order of priority**: serve in the IDF first (Israel enforces more strictly), then in Cyprus
- **Timeline**: you can serve in the IDF at 18-20 and in Cyprus at 21-22 (relevant only if the person is a Cypriot citizen by birth)
- **Legal advice**: you must consult a lawyer **in both countries** to coordinate

## What are the best strategies for timing citizenship?

### Strategy 1: postpone citizenship — the popular route

**The idea:** do not apply for Cypriot citizenship for the son until he is past the age of the service obligation.

| Parameter | Details |
|-------|-------|
| **Age at which the obligation ends** | 26 (for shortened service) or later |
| **Advantage** | No service, or only 3 months if he receives citizenship after 26 |
| **Disadvantage** | The son has no EU passport until 26+ and must renew residence permits |
| **Suited to** | Teenage sons (14+) who are not yet citizens |

**Important:** the son can live in Cyprus on a **residence permit** (as a dependant of the parents) without citizenship. This is legal and valid.

### Strategy 2: early citizenship + shortened service

**The idea:** give the son citizenship and treat the service as the "cost" of an EU passport.

| Parameter | Details |
|-------|-------|
| **Length of service** | 6 months (age 18-26) or 3 months (over 26) |
| **Advantage** | EU passport from a young age, freedom of movement, studying at EU fees |
| **Disadvantage** | Military service (short, but real), needs coordination with the IDF |
| **Suited to** | Families who want the EU passport early, sons who are willing to serve |

### Strategy 3: citizenship for daughters only

**The idea:** apply for citizenship only for the daughters, postpone it for the sons.

| Parameter | Details |
|-------|-------|
| **Advantage** | Daughters get the EU passport without service (Cyprus does not conscript women) |
| **Disadvantage** | The sons stay on residence permits — inequality within the family |
| **Suited to** | Families with daughters only, or who want to split |

> **Note:** in the IDF, daughters **are** required to serve (24 months). In Cyprus — they are **not**. This is a significant difference for planning.

## What practical scenarios should Israeli families consider?

### Scenario 1: a family with a 15-year-old son

**The situation:** the parents receive Cypriot citizenship. The son is 15 and studies at an international school in Limassol.

**The options:**

1. **Apply for citizenship for the son too** → he will have to serve at 18 (6 months as a naturalized citizen). Advantage: an EU passport before university = EU tuition fees across Europe.
2. **Postpone citizenship** → the son stays on a residence permit (as a dependant). He applies for citizenship after 26 → only 3 months of service. Disadvantage: "international" tuition fees at EU universities.

**Recommendation:** if the son plans to study in Europe, an EU passport is worth 6 months of service. The difference in tuition may reach €30,000–80,000 over a 3–4 year degree. To compare education costs: [Cost of living — Cyprus vs Israel](/en/life/cost-of-living-cyprus-vs-uk/).

### Scenario 2: a family with an 8-year-old son

**The situation:** the parents moved to Cyprus, the son is 8.

**The options:**

1. **Citizenship now** → the son will be a citizen from birth (for service purposes — 14 months). But: he is naturalized, not born in Cyprus, so **6 months** (shortened service for naturalized citizens).
2. **Wait** → give citizenship at 20+ = 6 months, or 26+ = 3 months.

**Recommendation:** at 8, there are another 10 years until conscription. If the family intends to stay, early citizenship gives long-term advantages (school, freedom of movement, belonging). 6 months of shortened service is reasonable.

### Scenario 3: a family with daughters only

**The situation:** two daughters, aged 12 and 16.

**The simple solution:** apply for citizenship for the whole family. Daughters are **not required to serve** in Cyprus. They get an EU passport with no military cost.

> **The Israeli side:** the daughters are still required to serve in the IDF (24 months) if they are Israeli citizens, unless there is an exemption (religious, medical, resident abroad). Service in Cyprus is irrelevant.

## What are the legal consequences of not serving in the Cypriot army?

### Enforcement

Cyprus **does enforce** the service obligation:

- **Draft notice** — sent to the registered address
- **Penalty for evasion** — up to **3 years in prison** or a fine
- **Travel restriction** — someone who has not fulfilled the obligation may be stopped on leaving Cyprus
- **Passport renewal problems** — there may be delays in renewing a Cypriot passport

### "Just don't come"

Some families think you can obtain citizenship and simply not come to Cyprus until the service age has passed. This is **risky**:

- Cyprus can restrict departure at the airport
- It may affect the status of the citizenship as a whole
- It infringes the citizen's rights under Protocol 4 of the European Convention on Human Rights

### Recommendation

**Do not try to get around the law.** If you decide not to serve — simply do not apply for citizenship. A residence permit (PR) gives most of the benefits without military duty.

## How does military service affect the parents' citizenship?

### A common question

"If our son does not receive citizenship — does that delay ours?"

**No.** A citizenship application is **personal**. Parents can naturalize without the children. Minor children are usually included in the parent's application, but **they can be left out of it** (this must be stated).

### Adult children (18+)

Children over 18 apply **independently**. They must meet the conditions themselves (years of residence, Greek, etc.). The parents' application does not affect theirs, and vice versa.

## How can you get a deferral or exemption from military service in Cyprus?

### Grounds for deferral

| Ground | Possible? | Notes |
|------|---------|-------|
| Studying abroad | Yes (usually) | Deferral for the period of study |
| Studying in Cyprus | No | Not a ground |
| Medical reasons | Yes | Military medical examination |
| Religious reasons | No | No religious exemption (unlike the IDF) |

### Exemption

A full exemption is granted only for **medical reasons** (a National Guard medical board) or in very exceptional circumstances.

## What to do — a decision checklist

### Step 1: map the family

- [ ] How many sons? What ages?
- [ ] How many daughters? (not required to serve in Cyprus)
- [ ] Are the sons Israeli citizens? (IDF obligation)
- [ ] Where are you in the immigration process? (PR? citizenship?)

### Step 2: get advice

- [ ] **Cypriot lawyer** — service obligation, deferrals, timing of citizenship
- [ ] **Israeli lawyer** — IDF obligation, resident-abroad exemption, coordination
- [ ] **If the son is 16+** — involve him in the decision. It is his life.

### Step 3: decide

- [ ] Citizenship for the whole family (including sons) — **if you are ready for service**
- [ ] Citizenship for parents and daughters, residence permit for sons — **if you want to postpone**
- [ ] Postpone citizenship for everyone — **if PR is enough**

---

The military service obligation in Cyprus is not a reason to give up EU citizenship — but it is an excellent reason to **plan the timing**. 6 months in the Cypriot army in exchange for an EU passport for life — for most families that is a good deal. But the decision is personal, and depends on the sons' ages and the family's plans.
