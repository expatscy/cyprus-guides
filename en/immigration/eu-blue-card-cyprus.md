> **Short answer:** Cyprus’s Blue Card threshold is **€43,632 gross per year**. The job must fall within an admitted sector and meet the qualification and contract requirements. A complete ordinary application has a 90-day decision timeframe; citizenship eligibility depends on the employer and the applicable naturalization conditions.

Cyprus began accepting Blue Card applications on **7 July 2025**. Admission is currently limited to **ICT, pharmaceuticals for research purposes, and shipping excluding captains and crew**. The minimum gross annual salary is **€43,632**; applicants also need qualifying professional credentials and a contract or binding offer for at least **six months**, alongside the other statutory conditions.

The Blue Card scheme covers **25 EU Member States**, excluding Denmark and Ireland. Mobility and employer changes are conditional. In Cyprus, changing employer requires an application: during the first 12 months, commencement of the new employment is suspended for 30 days; thereafter work can start upon submission. A Blue Card holder employed by a company covered by the government’s business-attraction Strategy may qualify for the highly skilled naturalization route, subject to all its conditions.

## What is the EU Blue Card?

### Definition

**EU Blue Card** is a unified European residence permit for highly qualified workers from third countries.

### Advantages

- **An EU scheme in 25 Member States**, excluding Denmark and Ireland
- Conditional **mobility to another participating EU country**
- Residence periods may count towards **EU long-term resident status**, subject to its conditions
- **Employer changes** under the applicable procedures

## What are the requirements?

### Salary

Minimum gross annual salary: **EUR 43,632**. Check the current official threshold and admitted sectors before applying.

The salary threshold is a legal admission criterion, not the only requirement.

### Education

- A qualifying higher education credential from a recognized institution: at least three years of study and the prescribed level
- Alternatively, for ICT services managers (ISCO 133) and ICT professionals (ISCO 25), relevant professional experience of at least three years within the seven years before applying

The evidence must relate to the work in the contract or offer.

### Employment Contract

- A contract or binding job offer from the specific Cyprus employer for highly qualified employment
- Follow the current official checklist for the required form and supporting evidence
- Minimum duration: **six months**

## How does the application process work?

### Where to Apply

Applications are submitted to the Migration Department’s **headquarters in Nicosia**.

- Check the official appointment instructions before attending

### Documents

Use the current [official Blue Card checklist](https://www.gov.cy/mip-md/en/documents/application-for-eu-blue-card/); the requirements depend on the application route and circumstances.

1. Evidence of the qualifying job contract or offer
2. Evidence of relevant professional qualifications
3. A valid travel document and the applicable entry/residence documentation
4. Sickness-insurance evidence where coverage or entitlement is not already provided through the work contract

### Supporting documents

Supporting documents must be officially translated and duly certified. Confirm which conditional checklist items apply to your case.

## How do I transition from a BCS visa?

### Is It Possible

A holder of a national permit for highly qualified employment may apply for a Blue Card, subject to the admission criteria. This is an application for the new route, not an automatic conversion.

### Why Transition

- **Blue Card** is a European standard
- Conditional mobility to a participating Member State
- Eligibility depends on the job and admission criteria, rather than a promise of unrestricted work for any company

### When to Transition

The national law provides for applications by holders of national highly skilled employment permits.

Apply when the statutory eligibility criteria and document requirements are met.
Check the long-term residence rules before assuming any particular period counts.

## How long does processing take?

Decision periods run from a complete application:

- Ordinary application: **within 90 days**
- Long-term mobility application: **within 30 days**
- Insufficient information or documents extend or suspend the applicable period; appointment waiting and card delivery are separate

## What does the Labour Department do?

The admission provisions require verification of the job and qualification documents by the Labour Department.

- Follow the applicable statutory and employer procedures
- Do not assume that an ICT job is automatically exempt from the required verification

## How do biometrics and registration work?

For an applicant arriving through the ordinary entry route:

1. Enter with the required visa or entry permit
2. Within **7 days**, register in the Aliens’ Register and submit the residence application or additional information required after an entry permit
3. Provide biometrics and signature at application or shortly afterwards, as instructed
4. If approved, follow the notice to collect the card

## What do families need to know?

Family reunification is available subject to the statutory conditions and supporting documents. The Blue Card rules waive a minimum prior residence period for the sponsor.

- A spouse must generally be at least 21 and the marriage at least one year old; ask about the dependent-visitor alternative if reunification conditions are temporarily unmet
- Eligible unmarried children under 18, subject to custody and maintenance conditions. Children aged 15–18 must not live independently; the national law provides their residence for other reasons rather than family reunification.
- Qualifying family permits can match the Blue Card’s duration, subject to travel-document validity

## How does Blue Card compare to BCS?

| Aspect | BCS | Blue Card |
|--------|-----|-----------|
| EU recognition | Cyprus national permit | 25 participating Member States; mobility conditions apply |
| EU relocation | National permit does not confer Blue Card mobility rights | Conditional mobility under the Blue Card rules |
| Changing employers | Follow national permit conditions | Apply for the change; first-12-month suspension applies |
| Company requirements | Check the relevant national scheme | Admitted job sector and statutory eligibility |
| Path to citizenship | Employer and applicant eligibility determine the route | Eligible Strategy employer may permit the highly skilled route |

### Important Note

**The residence-permit label alone does not determine the naturalization route.** The official Blue Card FAQ confirms that holders working for an employer covered by the business-attraction Strategy can benefit from the highly skilled provisions, subject to the full citizenship requirements.

## What practical tips should I know?

1. **Collect documents early** — especially degrees and employment references
2. **Confirm certification requirements** — use the official checklist before arranging legalization
3. **Check conditional documents** — use the current checklist for your route
4. **Compare service quotes** before deciding whether to appoint a representative
5. **Check appointment instructions** before travelling to Nicosia
