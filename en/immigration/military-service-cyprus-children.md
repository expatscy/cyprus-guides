> **Short answer:** Cyprus military obligations apply to male citizens, including naturalised citizens, with statutory exemptions and deferrals. The ordinary obligation begins on **1 January of the year of the 18th birthday**, rather than on the birthday itself. Reduced service requires the applicable age, parent-origin and enlistment conditions.

This guide follows the [current National Guard Law, 19(I)/2011](https://www.cylaw.org/nomoi/enop/non-ind/2011_1_19/full.html) and [National Guard instructions](https://www.gov.cy/army/sychnes-erotiseis/) checked on 3 October 2026. Ask the relevant recruitment office to determine your son's individual military status before planning study, travel or a citizenship application.

## Who must serve in the military?

Section 18 covers **male Cypriot citizens**, and also males with either parent of Cypriot origin who have not become Cypriot citizens, subject to the transitional exception in section 77(2). That exception concerns non-citizens of maternal Cypriot origin in already-called-up classes who had no service obligation under the repealed law; it ceases to protect them if they acquire Cypriot citizenship. Where an origin-based obligation applies, holding a residence permit or delaying a citizenship application does not by itself remove it.

The ordinary obligation runs from **1 January of the year in which the person turns 18 through 31 December of the year in which he turns 50**. Section 20 separately provides initial-service liability until age 45. Sections 18 and 22 allow emergency and specified reserve extensions beyond the ordinary age limit.

The law includes recognised conscientious-objector procedures for alternative military or social service, as well as exemption and deferral categories. Mandatory service does not mean that every covered person must undertake the same form or duration of service.

### Registration

Section 27 requires every male citizen to submit a declaration of personal military information **during the year he turns 16**. The National Guard's FAQ says submission is electronic through gov.cy. If citizenship is acquired later, contact the recruitment office to establish the correct registration and call-up procedure for the actual case.

## How long is service?

The [National Guard's current service page](https://www.gov.cy/army/thiteia/) describes **14 months for full-service conscripts**. Its [reduced-service page](https://www.gov.cy/army/meiomeni-thiteia/) explains that, following the reduction of full service to 14 months, the six- and three-month reduced categories remain relevant.

For the naturalisation categories in section 21, the applicable conditions include:

| Citizenship timing | Published statutory category and conditions |
|---|---|
| Acquired before the 18th birthday, with parents who are not of Cypriot origin | Section 21(1)(b)(ix) states 14 months, provided enlistment is immediately after recognised secondary-school graduation or within one year of acquiring citizenship; otherwise two additional months apply |
| Naturalisation after turning 18 and up to turning 26, with parents who are not of Cypriot origin | Section 21(1)(c)(iv): six months if enlisted within two years of naturalisation; otherwise two additional months apply |
| Naturalisation after turning 26, with parents who are not of Cypriot origin | Section 21(1)(d)(ii): three months if enlisted within two years of naturalisation; otherwise two additional months apply |

These conditions concern **the individual's citizenship acquisition and parents' origin**. They are not a blanket six- or three-month entitlement for all sons of naturalised parents. The extra two months arise from failure to meet the stated enlistment condition, rather than from every lawful deferral.

Reduced service is granted following an application and decision. Section 21(6) restricts reduced-service eligibility for specified officer, NCO and specially trained roles, subject to its exceptions. Other reduced-service categories, including qualifying permanent residents abroad who return to settle in Cyprus, have their own conditions; ask the recruitment office which category applies.

The reduced-service webpage's final naturalisation sentence is incomplete. The complete three-month conditions above come from section 21(1)(d)(ii) of the current law.

## What deferrals are available?

### School and university studies

Section 24 provides a deferral for pupils at **recognised secondary schools in Cyprus or abroad**, until completion of the basic course or interruption of attendance. The National Guard instructions describe annual school deferrals and the applicable school/individual submission procedures.

There is **no general university deferral until age 27**, whether the university is in Cyprus or abroad. The National Guard FAQ identifies specific education exceptions: students attending or selected for **nursing at a Cyprus higher-education institution**, or **cooking/pastry at a Cyprus further- or higher-education institution**. Section 24 permits the Minister to set a maximum number and selection criteria for those categories. Military-institution studies are another separately specified category. These examples are not exhaustive: section 75 also provides a minister-approved university-related deferral for its specified group of previously temporarily discharged conscripts who are reassessed as fit.

Ordinary university acceptance does not replace a military deferral. Obtain written confirmation of the applicable military status before agreeing a start date with the university.

### Medical grounds

The competent medical committees assess fitness. Under section 24 and the [National Guard's medical guidance](https://www.gov.cy/army/apallages-anavoles-krisi-somatikis-ikanotitas/), temporary inability to serve can lead to health deferrals totalling **up to two years**, granted in annual, six-month or three-month periods.

An **I/5** finding can result in temporary or permanent discharge for unfitness, depending on the assessment. Temporary discharge is not automatically a permanent exemption: the FAQ describes annual reassessment until age 40.

### Other grounds

Section 24 lists other categories, including a serving or simultaneously called-up brother under the stated conditions, hospitalisation, qualifying family relocation abroad, specified religious or public-service roles, and special family or financial circumstances. Each has its own conditions and duration. It is incorrect to say that only medical grounds qualify.

Apply through the relevant recruitment office or Ministry of Defence as instructed. Health deferrals are decided by the competent medical committees. Do not rely on a reason alone without the applicable decision.

## Does another passport or overseas residence exempt someone?

**Another nationality alone is not a blanket exemption.** Section 23 lists specific exemptions, including qualifying permanent residents abroad and certain completed foreign-service categories.

For example, section 23(1)(i) covers people **not of Cypriot origin** who completed military service **without buying it out in their country of origin before naturalisation**. Section 23(2) states that this does **not** exempt them from reserve service. Other foreign-service categories have different nationality, overseas-residence and service conditions.

Permanent-resident-abroad status has a detailed statutory definition in section 2, including the qualifying ten-year residence calculation, start dates and exclusions. Simply moving away or avoiding visits does not establish it. The [official exemption instructions](https://www.gov.cy/army/apallages-anavoles-krisi-somatikis-ikanotitas/) require an application and supporting evidence; a Cyprus embassy or consulate's permanent-residence certificate is the usual evidence for that category.

Under section 25, if the reason for an exemption or deferral ends, the person must notify the relevant recruitment office **in writing within one month**. Emergency provisions can also suspend or interrupt exemptions and deferrals.

## What happens if someone fails to attend or wants to travel?

Section 67 makes failure to comply with a lawful call-up or service order **without reasonable cause** an offence. On conviction, the maximum is **three years' imprisonment, a €6,000 fine, or both**. The offence is tied to the order and failure to comply; merely entering Cyprus is not its statutory trigger.

Section 70 prohibits departure by a conscript who has been called up unless the Minister grants special permission. It also permits the Minister to prohibit departure by a citizen aged 16 or above where departure is considered intended to avoid military obligations. Check the applicable exit-permit requirements before travelling.

The National Guard FAQ says an issued exit permit can be used within **30 days of issue**. It distinguishes people who have completed service but are under 26: they can travel using the specified reserve movement document or a **Type B military-status certificate** instead of an exit permit.

## How should a family plan citizenship?

Check the son's age, parents' origin, date and legal route of citizenship acquisition, residence history, existing call-up, and any approved exemption or deferral. Keep the citizenship certificate and the relevant military correspondence.

A passport application and citizenship acquisition are different events. Immigration permission while an application is pending must be assessed under its own rules; a visitor permit should not be treated as a guaranteed solution for every adult child.

Citizenship applications also depend on the person's route and circumstances. Use the [standard naturalisation guide](/en/immigration/cyprus-citizenship-naturalization-standard/) or [highly skilled route](/en/immigration/cyprus-citizenship-naturalization/) for their separate residence, eligibility and fee requirements. Do not apply a generic adult-child form, salary threshold or military-service fee table to every case.

For individual military questions, use the [National Guard FAQ's recruitment-office contacts](https://www.gov.cy/army/sychnes-erotiseis/). Obtain advice from a lawyer where the facts or an outstanding obligation require it.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do children of naturalised citizens have to serve in the Cyprus army?

Male Cypriot citizens fall within the National Guard Law, subject to its exemptions and deferrals. Reduced service depends on the person's age when citizenship is acquired, parents' Cypriot origin and enlistment conditions. Do not assume that every naturalised citizen's son qualifies for six or three months.

### Can military service be deferred in Cyprus?

Yes, for the statutory categories. These include recognised secondary-school pupils, specified nursing or cooking/pastry studies in Cyprus, medical grounds and other listed circumstances. Ordinary university admission in Cyprus or abroad does not create a general deferral until age 27. Obtain the applicable approval before relying on a deferral.

### From what age is military service compulsory in Cyprus?

The ordinary statutory obligation runs from 1 January of the year a covered male turns 18 through 31 December of the year he turns 50. Initial-service liability is separately limited to age 45, and emergency or specified reserve extensions can apply. Male citizens must submit their personal military information in the year they turn 16.
